# How to work on this site

This repository is a small website of interactive physics revision activities for Alex's students, hosted on GitHub Pages. Each activity is one self-contained HTML page. `index.html` is the launch page in two sections. "Physics Workout (Students)" has Year 9, IGCSE, IAL and IB, which open `year-9.html`, `igcse.html`, `ial.html` and `ib.html`, the workout lists for that course (`ib.html` is grouped by IB theme, e.g. Theme E with `quantum-physics.html`, E.2 Quantum Physics, HL only; `ial.html` (2 Nov 2026) so far holds only its "Core Practicals" section, `#unit-cp`, with the IAL practical pages CP7 `resistivity-practical.html`, CP8 `internal-resistance-practical.html` (14 Nov 2026), CP12 `thermistor-practical.html`, CP13 `latent-heat-practical.html`, CP14 `boyles-law-practical.html` and CP15 `gamma-absorption-practical.html`). ## Before you start any task

- **Purpose (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** Every workout, animation and practical page is there to help students learn for their exams (4PH1, IAL, IB) in an interesting, interactive way. Judge every idea and every review against both aims:
  - **Exam value:** which spec point or exam skill does it build (exam wording, a calculation, a graph, a practical method)?
  - **Interest:** what does the student have to think about or do? It must not be a worksheet on a screen, or a game that tests something other than the physics.
  When suggesting ideas, say how each one meets both. When reviewing, give every activity a one-line verdict on each, and cut or rework anything weak on either.
- **Most activities are for Edexcel IGCSE Physics (4PH1).** Pitch a new activity at IGCSE level unless the request says otherwise. If the request suggests a different group (e.g. KS3), confirm the year group first (see "Pitching it" below), as it changes the whole page.
- **Exam boards (Alex, 20 Oct 2026).** Alex teaches Edexcel only: IGCSE is Pearson Edexcel 4PH1, A Level is Pearson Edexcel International A Level Physics (IAL, 2018 spec), plus the IB Diploma (first assessment 2025). Check content against those official specifications (qualifications.pearson.com, the IB guide and data booklet), never against other boards' specs or revision sites. Edexcel IAL Physics has no coefficient of friction (that is A Level Maths mechanics); IB A.2 has F<sub>f</sub> ≤ μ<sub>s</sub>F<sub>N</sub> and F<sub>f</sub> = μ<sub>d</sub>F<sub>N</sub>.
- **Copy from the existing pages, don't start from scratch.** Every workout (Year 9 and IGCSE, 21 Oct 2026) runs on the same `act()` engine, and its copies are identical on every page: `act`, `bar`, `track`, `makeDraggable`, `keyActivate`, `celebrate`, `createBlankGame` (cloze, units table, graph labels, diagram labels; `palette:true` copies chips instead of using them up), `createSortGame`, `createMatchGame`, `createMemoryGame`, `createCalc`, `createReorder`, `createPredict`, `createSpot`, `createCrossword` (2 Nov 2026: a typed crossword; positions come from `tools/crossword/layout.js`, used on the Unit 4 Units and Key Terms page for 4a and 4b together), `lineGraph`, `frameAct`, the `FR()` fraction helper, `.act-pair`, `.sim-small` and the side-by-side layouts below. Copy the engine from `density-pressure-state.html` and change only the data; a fix to the engine goes into every copy (and `tools/practical-gen/template/engine.js`). The Unit 1 pages add small page builders on top (`clozeAct`, `unitsAct`, `calcAct`, `spotAct`, `labelGraphAct`, `mgraph` for section dividers and a dashed second line); copy those from `motion.html`.
- **Commit straight to `main`** unless Alex asks for a branch. Pushing to `main` updates the live site in a minute or two.
- **When students are using the site** (Alex says not to push live), commit to a working branch instead and push only that branch. Keep a list of what is waiting, and merge it into `main` in one go when Alex says "go live".

## Site structure

- One HTML file per activity in the repository root (teaching animations go in `animations/` instead), named in lowercase with hyphens (e.g. `motion.html`, `heat-transfer.html`, `gases.html`).
- **Breadcrumb header (Alex, 21 Oct 2026).** Every workout and practical starts with one compact line: a breadcrumb `<nav class="eyebrow crumbs" aria-label="Breadcrumb">` where each level is a link (Year 9: "Workouts › Year 9 ›" to `index.html` and `year-9.html`; IGCSE: "Workouts › IGCSE › Unit n ›", the unit linking to `igcse.html#unit-n`; practicals: "… › Core practicals ›" to `igcse.html#unit-cp`; IB: "Workouts › IB › Theme E ›" to `ib.html` and `ib.html#theme-e`), then a small `<h1>` (22–30px), the "N of M tabs done" pill (the engine puts it in `.page-head`), and on pages with Triple Award content `<span class="head-note">TA = Triple Award only</span>`. No separate back link and no eyebrow line. A `.key` legend or `.cp-link` line wraps onto its own row below. The CSS block is "compact breadcrumb header" (copy it from `motion.html`; the practical template has it in `style.css`). On phones (≤640px) the tabs are one row that swipes sideways, and `applyTabUI` scrolls the open tab into view. The course lists (`year-9.html`, `igcse.html`) keep their back link to `index.html`.
- When you add a new page, also add its card to the right course list (`year-9.html`, `igcse.html` or `ib.html`): title ending in "Workout", and one line saying what students do. IGCSE titles start with the unit number (e.g. "1a Forces and Motion Workout"), and `igcse.html` lists cards in unit-number order, not by year.
- **How big a workout is (Alex, 16 Oct 2026).** One IGCSE workout per spec sub-section (the spec's own lettered blocks, e.g. 1(b) Movement and position), merging a small sub-section into its neighbour so no page has fewer than 5 tabs. **5–8 short tabs per page, 1 or 2 activities on each (Alex, 5 Nov 2026)**, each page starting with its Warm Up tab and ending with its Sum Up tab (see "Warm Up and Sum Up" below): many small tabs beat a few full ones. **15–30 minutes per workout (Alex, 5 Nov 2026):** the content tabs come to 15–30 minutes in all, not counting the unit's Units and Key Terms page (about 10 minutes more). Core practicals are never part of a workout (see "Core practicals"). **What the 2 really limits (Alex, 11 Nov 2026):** activities a student cannot see without scrolling below the first screen. Two small activities side by side (an `.act-pair`, e.g. a Predict and a Sort, or two Orders) count as one row, so a tab may hold 3 or 4 when they sit two to a row and the tab still fits about one laptop screen (1366×657). A third activity that would sit out of sight below the others is split into its own tab, or the weakest activity is cut. (About 8–16 activities per page; Calculate cards have 2 questions (see "Calculate questions" under Equations), sorts 6–8 cards, matches 4–6 pairs.) Cut KS3-level items and anything a second activity already tests before cutting calculations, which are the most exam-useful. A tab's short multiple-choice questions go in one Quick check card, not a row of single Predicts. **Units and Key Terms: one page per unit (Alex, 12 Nov 2026; before that two tabs on the unit's last non-TA workout).** Each unit's whole-unit Units and Key Terms activities live on their own page, `unit-n-units-and-terms.html`, titled "Unit n Units and Key Terms Workout", with three tabs (two on Unit 5): "Units" (id `units`, heading "Unit n Units": one quantity–symbol–unit activity, plus any unit-conversion activity; no equations-in-words activity (Alex, 11 Nov 2026: the equations are given in the exam)) then "Key Terms" (id `keywords`, heading "Unit n Key Terms": a Match listing every crossword word with its meaning; Alex, 12 Nov 2026: its own tab, the place students check when stuck) then "Crossword" (id `terms`, heading "Unit n Crossword": the unit's one crossword, 8–13 words with TA-only words marked "(TA)" in the clue; the match and the crossword hold the same words, and only real key words (Alex, 12 Nov 2026): a term students must define or use in an answer, not a part, a device, a unit or a verb, so no National Grid, core, turbine or becquerel); Unit 5 has no crossword, so its "Key Terms" tab (id `terms`) is its key-terms match. ## Page shell

- Order: `<title>`, Google Fonts links, one `<style>` block, then `<div class="page">` holding a `<header class="page-head">` (breadcrumb + `<h1>`, no intro paragraph; see "Breadcrumb header"), a `<nav class="tabs" role="tablist">` with 1–2 word tab labels, then one `<section id="panel-{id}" class="panel" hidden>` per activity (the first tab has no `hidden`), then a single `<script>` containing all data, helpers, factories and wiring.

In match games whose left items are short terms, give the left column less width (e.g. `minmax(0,1fr) minmax(0,2.6fr)` on wide screens) so each definition fits on one line and the whole list fits on screen. No `max-width` on intro paragraphs. Small decorative diagrams stay small (≤130px) so the activity is never pushed below the fold. Must work at phone width (about 380px) with no sideways scrolling.

## How activities behave

- **Drag and drop** uses custom mouse and touch events (not HTML5 drag and drop) via `makeDraggable`, with a 6px threshold to tell a tap from a drag.
- **Tentative, then Check.** Never mark an answer on drop. A "Check" button reveals results: correct ones lock and turn green; wrong ones shake, then return to the pool after 700–800ms. Students must be able to get it wrong and see it. **Show answer (Alex, 4 Nov 2026):** after a Check with a wrong answer, every marked activity (fill in the blanks, units tables, labels, sort, match, order, crossword, plot) shows a "Show answer" button (`answerBtn()` after `bar()` in the engine). It fills in and locks the right answers, dashed (`.is-shown`) rather than green, and counts as done. A Calculate question never opens its working by itself: a wrong answer gives that question its own Show answer button, which puts the answer in the box and opens the working. Predicts already show the answer after a second wrong try. **Fill in (Alex, 5 Nov 2026):** every `createBlankGame` activity (fill-in-the-blanks passages, units tables, graph and diagram labels) has a Check button, Shuffle again and Fill in (`createBlankGame({fill:true,…})`); Fill in is always visible, does what Show answer does, and replaces Show answer on those activities. Passages label their Check button "Check answers".
- **One click empties a slot.** Clicking any filled, unlocked slot sends its chip straight back. Dropping a new chip on a filled slot replaces it.
- **Reorder** updates the order live while dragging and also has ▲▼ buttons. Check gives a score, not per-row marks.
- **Don't give the answer away.** Labels and chips describe the physics meaning ("Constant velocity, backwards"), never a visual feature the student can match by eye ("sloping down"). Ask: could a student get this right by matching words instead of understanding? If so, reword it.
- **Keyboard and screen readers.** Every chip, card and slot is a `<button>` (or has `tabindex="0"` and `role="button"`). Select-then-target works with Enter and Space as well as clicks. Keep a visible `:focus-visible` outline. Announce Check results in an `aria-live="polite"` region (e.g. "6 / 8 correct"). Touch targets are at least 44px tall.

## Animation

Animation is feedback, not decoration. CSS transitions and `@keyframes` only.

- Correct: brief scale to 1.05 and back with the green fading in (about 250ms). Wrong: `shake` (about 500ms), then the bounce back. Chip settling into a slot: 150ms ease-out. New panel: 250ms fade-in. Predict-then-reveal outcomes: 400–600ms. Finished activity: one brief celebration, such as the score pill pulsing, never full screen.
- Animate only `transform` and `opacity`, so it runs smoothly on old Chromebooks and phones.
- **Respect reduced motion.** Put movement inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`. Under `reduce`, swap the shake for a colour change and slides or flips for an instant change. The meaning must still come through.
- Nothing loops forever, and nothing flashes more than three times a second.

## Equations

- **Never use ÷ in an equation.** Show every division as a stacked fraction: write `[[top|bottom]]` in the text and pass it through the page's `FR()` helper (see `density-pressure-state.html`), which draws the fraction and keeps numbers like "15 000" on one line. This applies to Spot the Error steps, Calculate intros and "Show full working", the equation readouts beside sliders, Predict feedback and Order steps. For screen readers, a fraction reads as "top over bottom".
- **Show every step, never jump to it.** Where a readout or worked answer uses a rearranged form, write the equation as learnt, then each step of the algebra on its own line, then the numbers (e.g. m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v → 2 × 3 + 1 × 0 = (2 + 1) × v → 6 = 3 × v → v = [[6|3]] → v = 2 m/s). Don't add notes like "divide both sides by 3": the lines themselves show the algebra. For braking distance from v² = u² + 2as, state v = 0 and acceleration −a, then 0 = u² − 2as → 2as = u² → s = [[u²|2a]].
- **Motion: use the graph.** Wherever a motion graph is shown (an animation, an explore, a Calculate question), find the answers from it: speed or acceleration as the gradient of the right stretch (read the two points off the graph), and distance as the area under the velocity–time graph (rectangle, triangle, ½ × base × height). Don't use v² = u² + 2as or other equations of motion there; they belong in the "Using v² = u² + 2as" tab and in questions with no graph.
- **Line up the = signs** in every working readout, so each line reads left | = | right like a worked answer on paper (`watchEquals()` does this automatically for `.eq`, `.xs-eq` and `.fma-eq`). Keep explanations of *why an answer is wrong* in Spot the Error and Predict feedback; those are not algebra notes.
- **Calculate working is two columns (Alex, 5 Nov 2026).** Write a Calculate question's `working` as `"givens||steps"`: the known values down the left, one per line (`s = 18 m`, a unit conversion on its own line, `t = 10 min = 600 s`), then the unknown as `a = ?`; after `||`, the equation as learnt, the numbers substituted, each rearranging step, then the answer. `createCalc` lines up each column on its = signs and stacks them on phones. A pure read-off with no equation can stay one column. Models: the 1c suvat card and the 2a P = I × V card.
- **Calculate questions (Alex, 11 Nov 2026).** A Calculate card normally has **2 questions**, and New problems gives a new pair with different values and different properties (the context, the material or object, and which quantity is the unknown). **Question 1 always needs a rearrangement**: the unknown is never the subject of the equation as learnt (find m or V from ρ = m/V, never ρ). **Question 2 is harder**: a power or root (r in Snell's law with sin⁻¹, v from KE = ½mv² or v² = u² + 2as), a unit change (minutes to seconds, cm² to m², g to kg), a prefix (k, M, m, G), a minus sign, or two equations in a row; give it `hints`. Write each question as a generator `rnd=>({q,unit,answer,working,hints})` that first picks a variant (which quantity is unknown) and a context, then the values from lists (`calcPick`) so answers are tidy, and build the card with `calcFix(gen, seed)`, which makes the first (fixed) set from the generator and adds it as `make` (e.g. `calcAct(tab,title,intro,[calcFix(q1,1),calcFix(q2,2)])`; models: 1a Speed and acceleration, 1d Balance the beam with its diagram drawn inside each question so New problems redraws it). Write numbers with `calcNum()` (no float noise, real minus sign, spaced thousands) and give a needed `a`/`an` from the number (an 8 kg, an 18 V). Cards whose numbers come from one fixed graph, table, trace or animation (Read the graph, Work out the numbers, Resistance from the graph, stopping distance, the nuclear-equation balances) are exempt and keep their questions. The engine saves a signature of each card's questions and generators with its answers, so a changed card never loads old answers into new boxes: no `STORE_KEY` bump is needed for a Calculate change. Every Calculate question has a `make: rnd=>({q, unit, answer, working})` that writes a new version testing the same skill (same equation, rearrangement and unit conversion), with values chosen from lists (`calcPick(rnd, […])`) so answers are tidy and sensible, and varied contexts and names. The card then shows a New problems button; the first set is always the fixed questions, and the seed is saved with the answers. A question whose numbers come from a fixed graph, table, diagram or animation gets no `make`. **Hints on demand (6 Nov 2026):** give a harder multi-step question `hints:[…]` (one step per hint, through `FR()`, and from `make` too); `createCalc` then shows a "Hint (1 of 3)" button under it that reveals one more hint per press (model: 4c Energy and Power); pages that use it need the `.calc-hint-row`/`.calc-hints` CSS from `work-and-power.html`. Check every page with `python3 tools/calc-check.py page.html` (local server on port 8765), then read some generated problems yourself: the checker only checks they are consistent, not that the physics is right.

## Pitching it

- **Primary (about 7–11):** 3–5 tabs, one activity each; 4–6 items per sort or match; short words; pictures on chips; chips at least 48px tall with 16px+ text; warmer success animation; no scores that compare pupils.
- **KS3 (11–14):** the Heat Transfer page's level; 6–10 items per activity.
- **GCSE/IGCSE and above:** the Forces page's level; exam-style wording, numeric questions, graphs, units tables.
- Keep wording at the class's reading level. Every scenario should be a plausible exam phrasing, not a giveaway. If a test or scheme of work is shared, use it only to see which skills to target; never copy its questions.

## Group activities

For team tasks (jigsaws, escape rooms), design so the group genuinely has to cooperate: each member holds information the others need, and answers are checked in a way that can't be guessed or copied from one person.
