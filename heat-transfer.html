Which mechanism does each one reduce?

The six features above all boil down to three underlying tricks — and some block more than one mechanism at once.

Trapped air

Reduces conduction & convection

Air is a poor conductor, and trapping it stops it from flowing, which also stops convection currents forming.

Reflective foil

Reduces radiation

The shiny surface reflects infrared radiation instead of absorbing it, cutting down the energy transferred by radiation.

Vacuum

Reduces conduction & convection

With almost no particles present, there's nothing to conduct energy through, and nothing to move and form a convection current.

Matt & dark, or shiny & light?

Dark, matt surfaces absorb and emit radiation well. Shiny, light surfaces reflect it instead. Read each example and drag it into the box that explains what's really happening.

How to answer a heat transfer question

Use these five steps for any "describe", "explain" or "suggest" question on this topic.

1

Where does the energy start, and where does it end up?

Find the hot source and the object or substance receiving the energy.

2

Which way does the energy move, and why?

Heat always moves from hot to cold — check which side is which.

3

What method carries the energy?

Conduction, convection, radiation, or evaporation — there might be more than one.

4

How does that method move the energy?

Say what the particles, the substance, or the radiation are actually doing.

5

Now practise it yourself.

Use steps 1–4 on the two questions below, then click to check your answer.

Example A · one method

On a cold day, the inside of a brick wall is warmer than the outside. Explain how thermal energy is transferred through the wall.

Check your answer
  • Energy transfers from the warm inside surface to the cold outside surface.
  • This is because the inside is at a higher temperature than the outside.
  • The mechanism is conduction, because the wall is a solid.
  • Particles on the warm inner surface vibrate more and collide with their neighbours, passing energy along the wall until the outer surface warms up.

Example B · two methods

A pan of soup is heated on an electric hob. Explain how thermal energy is transferred from the hob to the top of the soup.

Check your answer
  • Energy transfers from the hob to the base of the pan by conduction.
  • Particles in the hob vibrate and pass energy to the pan's particles, which pass it on to the soup touching the base.
  • The heated soup at the bottom expands, becomes less dense, and rises.
  • Cooler, denser soup sinks to take its place near the base and is heated in turn.
  • This convection current carries the energy up through the soup until it's all heated through.
