IB Animations

Theme A Space, Time and Motion

Motion Graphs

Drive a car forwards and backwards and watch its displacement–time, velocity–time and acceleration–time graphs draw together: gradients give v and a, areas give Δs and Δv. Predict: given one graph of a journey, choose the sketch of another (A.1).

Projectile Lab

Launch up to three projectiles at once: velocity components, motion graphs, air resistance, and suvat working for the flight time, maximum height and range.

Shoot the Monkey

Fire a dart rifle from the shoulder at a monkey that lets go: aimed straight at it, at any angle or horizontally, both fall ½gt², so it still hits. Set the angle yourself to see it miss.

Free-Body Diagrams

Draw the forces on an object in 18 situations, from a book on a table to a ball hanging in an accelerating car, then check directions and relative sizes.

Newton's Laws

A puck on ice (first law), Zahra on scales in a lift (second law) and two skaters pushing apart (third law), each with two predict questions.

Skydiver and Terminal Speed

A skydiver jumps, then opens her parachute: the resultant force F = Fg − Fd and a = F/m at every moment, with her acceleration–time graph under her velocity–time graph. Predict the air resistance or her acceleration at a point on the graph (A.1, A.2).

Friction

Pull a block with a rising force: static friction grows up to μsFN, then drops to μdFN once it slides, with the Ff–Fapp graph and an Experiment mode. Plus the magnified surfaces, dry and lubricated.

Inclined Plane

A block on a ramp: resolve its weight, compare static and dynamic friction, and watch it slide, stop or stay put, with a velocity–time graph.

Momentum: Collisions and Explosions

Two trolleys stick together, bounce apart or are pushed apart by a spring: find v2 from conservation of momentum, then compare the kinetic energy before and after: elastic, inelastic or an explosion. Impulse on each trolley (A.2).

Hooke’s Law

Load a spring, a metal wire or a rubber band: the spring constant k from the gradient of the force–extension graph (FH = −kx) and the elastic potential energy EH = ½k(Δx)² as the area under it. Experiment mode (A.2, A.3).

Falling Ball: Viscosity

A steel ball falls through a viscous liquid: weight, buoyancy Fb = ρVg and viscous drag Fd = 6πηrv, terminal velocity, and a free-body diagram to complete. Experiment mode: find the viscosity from v against r² (A.2).

Torques on a Seesaw

Rotational equilibrium: clockwise and counter-clockwise torques with the plank’s own weight acting at its centre of gravity and a pivot you can move off centre (A.4, HL only).

Theme B The Particulate Nature of Matter

Specific Heat Capacity

Heat water or a metal block with an immersion heater: energy from V × I × t and Q = mcΔT, with an Experiment mode that finds c from the gradient of the T–t graph.

Gas Molecules and Pressure

Molecules hit the walls of a cylinder: change T, V, N and the gas and see pV = NkBT, the spread of molecular kinetic energies, the mean Ek = &frac32;kBT, internal energy and absolute zero. Derivation: build P = ⅓ρv̄² and Ēk = &frac32;kBT a line at a time, from one molecule hitting a wall.

Current–Voltage Characteristics

Trace the I–V graphs of a fixed resistor, a filament lamp and a diode from −6 V to +6 V on a circuit built on the bench, and compare R = V/I with the gradient at a point. Experiment mode, and a Build tab for the meters.

Series and Parallel Circuits

Series, parallel and combined circuits built on the bench beside the circuit diagram: put the meters in yourself, current conserved at junctions, p.d. sharing, real meters that change what they measure (a 20 Ω voltmeter), and Build tasks that show what a meter in the wrong place does.

Resistivity of a Wire

Drag a crocodile clip along a wire on a metre rule: R = ρL/A, the R–L graph with a small contact-resistance intercept, and ρ = gradient × A. Experiment mode.

Potential Divider

Vout = R2/(R1 + R2) × Vin with fixed resistors, an LDR, an NTC thermistor or a potentiometer, built on the bench beside the circuit diagram; a lamp or alarm at 3.0 V, the effect of a load, and a Build tab (design a night light or a fire alarm).

E.m.f. and Internal Resistance

A battery, a car battery or a potato cell, each with internal resistance, and a rheostat you drag: terminal p.d. V = ε − Ir, lost volts, open and short circuit, and the V–I line (intercept ε, gradient −r). Experiment mode.

The National Grid

Build the grid with step-up and step-down transformers, send the power, then work out the power wasted in the cables (P = I²R) and the efficiency.

Black Body Radiation: Wien’s Law

A star beside its black body emission spectrum: change the surface temperature and watch λmax and the colour change (Wien’s displacement law, B.1). Questions on the curve, and an Experiment mode: scan a mystery star’s spectrum and find its temperature.

Theme C Wave Behaviour

Red-Shift: the Doppler Effect for Light

Set a galaxy’s velocity and watch its wavefronts and the lines in its spectrum shift, with the working for Δλ/λ ≈ v/c (C.5). Questions: which way the galaxy is moving, its speed from a shift in wavelength or in frequency, and the wavelength received.

Theme D Fields

Alpha, Beta and Gamma in Fields

Fire alpha, beta and gamma through a magnetic or electric field: which way each bends, the beta fan, and r = p/BQ for alpha and beta.

Magnet and Coil: Lenz’s Law

Push a magnet into a coil: what changes the size of the induced emf, then predict which way the induced current flows with Lenz’s law (D.4, HL only).

Theme E Nuclear and Quantum Physics

Photoelectric Effect

Light on a metal plate: threshold frequency, Emax = hf − Φ, and the stopping voltage on the current–voltage graph (IB E.2).

Electron Diffraction

Electrons through thin graphite make rings on a screen: raise the voltage and the rings shrink as λ = h/p gets shorter. Electron count against angle, and an Experiment mode where D against 1/√V gives the atom spacing (IB E.2).

Film Badge: Alpha, Beta or Gamma?

Predict which patches of a film badge go dark for an alpha, beta, gamma or mixed source, then expose it. IB extras: dose and beta energy.

Random Decay and Half-Life

Nuclei decay one by one at random: watch N halve every half-life on the N–t graph. IB extras: the ln N–t graph, the decay constant and activity.

Chain Reaction in a Nuclear Reactor

U-235 fuel rods, a moderator and boron control rods: raise and lower the rods and watch the chain reaction die out, stay steady (k = 1) or grow on a graph of fissions per generation, with an Experiment mode.

The Life Cycle of a Star

Follow a star across the HR diagram, from the main sequence to a white dwarf, or to a supernova: the star drawn to scale, lines of constant radius, the instability strip and its radius from L = σAT4 (E.5). Questions on the diagram: plot a star, draw its evolutionary path, work out a radius.

Stellar Parallax

The Earth orbits the Sun and a nearby star moves against the distant stars: drag the star and see the parallax angle change, with d (parsec) = 1/p (arc-second) and the distance in light years (E.5). Questions on the picture: distance from the shift, unit conversions, and two stars compared.
