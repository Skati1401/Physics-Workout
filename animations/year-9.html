Year 9 Animations

8L Earth and Space

Day, Night and a Year

The Earth spins once every 24 hours and orbits the Sun once a year. Pin a city and watch it turn into day and night, then speed up a whole year of 365 spins. Then answer questions on the picture.

Phases of the Moon

Drag the Moon round the Earth and see how it looks from the UK. The Sun always lights half of the Moon; the phases are how much of that lit half faces us. Then predict the phase for a position, and the position for a phase.

Seasons

The Earth goes round the Sun with its axis tilted at 23.5°. See the length of the day, the height of the midday Sun and how spread out its rays are in the UK, New Zealand and at the North Pole, and why the seasons are not caused by the distance from the Sun.

Changing Models of the Solar System

Ptolemy’s Earth-centred model, Copernicus’s circles round the Sun and Kepler’s ellipses, with their dates. Watch Mars loop backwards across the sky in each model, and the moons of Jupiter that Galileo saw. Then match each model to its scientist and decide which evidence supports which model.

Orbits and Gravity

A planet, the Moon, a comet and an artificial satellite in their orbits, with the pull of gravity and the direction of motion. Turn the Sun’s gravity off and the planet flies off in a straight line. Then predict the path when gravity stops, and compare a comet’s orbit with a planet’s.

Mass and Weight on Other Worlds

An astronaut stands on scales on the Earth, the Moon and the planets. Her mass stays the same but her weight changes with the gravitational field strength. Jump on each world, then work out weights with weight = mass × gravitational field strength.

Scale of the Solar System

The planets at their true sizes side by side, the 1 : 350 000 000 scale model across London, and a calculator for ratios, fractions and percentages. Then simplify ratios to 1 : n and write comparisons as percentages.
