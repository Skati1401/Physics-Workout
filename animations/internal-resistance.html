Why plot V against I?
ε = I(R + r)
ε = IR + Ir
ε = V + Ir
V = ε − Ir
V = −r × I + ε
V=−r × I+εy=m × x+c

IR is the p.d. across the rheostat, V, which is what the voltmeter reads. Matching V = −rI + ε to y = mx + c: plot V up the side and I along the bottom and you get a straight line. Its gradient is −r and its y-intercept is ε. The intercept is where I = 0 (open circuit): no current, no lost volts, so V = ε.
