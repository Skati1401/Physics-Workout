IGCSE Animations

Unit 1 Forces and Motion

Motion Graphs

Drive a car and watch its distance–time and velocity–time graphs draw, with gradients and areas worked out.

A Toy Car on a Ramp

Core practical 1.5

Release a toy car down a ramp, time it with a stopwatch or light gates, and plot its distance–time and velocity–time graphs.

Force, Mass and Acceleration

Push a trolley loaded with masses with a resultant force and see how force and mass change its acceleration, with its velocity–time graph.

Friction

Magnified surfaces rubbing over each other, dry and with a lubricant.

Skydiver

Weight, air resistance and the velocity–time graph, from the jump to two terminal velocities. Then predict the air resistance arrow at a point on the graph.

Reaction Time: Sheep or Car

Measure your reaction time two ways: dart five sheep as each one bolts and find the mean, or brake a car when the light turns red and see its thinking, braking and stopping distance on a velocity–time graph.

Stopping Distance

Two cars stop side by side: tired or distracted drivers, wet or icy roads, and the areas under their velocity–time graphs.

Momentum: Collisions and Explosions

Two trolleys stick together, bounce apart or are pushed apart by a spring (an explosion); total momentum before equals total momentum after.

Hooke’s Law

Core practical 1.22

Load a spring, a metal wire or a rubber band and watch the force–extension graph, past the limit of proportionality.

Moments on a Seesaw

Change two weights and their distances until the clockwise and anticlockwise moments balance, then predict which side goes down or where to sit to balance.

Unit 2 Electricity

Series and Parallel Circuits

Series and parallel circuits built on the bench beside the circuit diagram: put the ammeters and voltmeters in yourself, watch the current split and rejoin at junctions, and see how the p.d. is shared. Build tasks show what goes wrong when a meter is in the wrong place.

Current–Voltage Characteristics

Core practical 2.9

Play sweeps the voltage from −6 V to +6 V and traces the I–V graph of a fixed resistor, a filament lamp and a diode, on a circuit built on the bench. Experiment mode: take readings and plot them. Build: put the ammeter and the voltmeter in yourself.

LDRs and Thermistors in a Potential Divider

An LDR or a thermistor in a potential divider, built on the bench: more light or a higher temperature lowers its resistance, so the output voltage changes and a lamp or alarm switches. Build: design a night light or a fire alarm.

Charging by Friction

Core practical 2.23P

Rub polythene, acetate or metal rods with a cloth, then bring them near a hanging charged rod, paper, water or an electroscope. Predict what happens on the picture, then try it. Experiment mode: record every pair and find the two types of charge.

Unit 3 Waves

Refraction of Light

Core practicals 3.17 and 3.19

A ray box shines through a rectangular block, a semicircular block or a prism: measure i and r, find the critical angle, see total internal reflection and dispersion. Experiment mode: trace rays and find n from sin i against sin r.

Speed of Sound in Air

Core practical 3.25P

Time a bang across a field, time echoes from a wall, or read two microphones on an oscilloscope. Experiment mode: take three timings at each distance and plot distance against time.

Frequency of a Sound Wave

Core practical 3.27P

A signal generator or tuning fork, a microphone and an oscilloscope: set the time base and Y-gain, get a steady trace and read T and f. Experiment mode: measure mystery tuning forks with two cursors.

Unit 4 Energy Resources and Energy Transfers

Energy on a Roller Coaster

Change the mass, start speed, friction and heights: GPE, kinetic energy and work done against friction always add up to the same total.

Thermal Energy Transfer

Core practical 4.9

Pins drop off conducting rods, dye shows a convection current, and a Leslie cube compares the infrared from four surfaces.

Unit 5 Solids, Liquids and Gases

Measuring Density

Core practical 5.4

Measure a regular block, an irregular stone and a liquid: mass on a balance, volume by ruler or displacement, then plot mass against volume.

Heating Curve and Particles

Core practical 5.11P

Heat ice until it becomes steam: the temperature–time graph and the particles move together.

Specific Heat Capacity

Core practical 5.14P

Heat water or a metal block with an immersion heater and joulemeter, and compare how much each warms up.

Gas Molecules and Pressure

Molecules hit the walls of a cylinder; change the temperature and volume, then see the gas-law graphs.

Pressure in a Liquid

Water pushing on a dam gets stronger with depth, and a U-tube manometer measures gas pressure.

Unit 6 Magnetism and Electromagnetism

Magnetic Field Patterns

Core practical 6.6

Sprinkle iron filings round one magnet, like poles or unlike poles, then check with field lines and a plotting compass.

Fields Around Wires and Coils

Sprinkle iron filings round a straight wire, one loop, a flat coil and a solenoid, then reverse the current and check with a plotting compass.

Electromagnets and Circuit Breakers

Pick up paper clips with an electromagnet (TA): change the current, the turns and the core, then switch off. Then overload a circuit breaker (TA) and watch it trip.

The Motor Effect

Predict on the picture which way a current-carrying wire, a rod on rails or a wire swing moves in a magnetic field, then press Go. Practise Fleming’s left-hand rule on exam-style diagrams, for wires and for charged particles (TA).

DC Motor and Generator

Step a motor’s coil round a turn at a time, then run it backwards as a generator and watch the meter and the output graph.

Magnet and Coil

Push a magnet into a coil and pull it out: what makes the induced voltage bigger, and what reverses it. Then predict which way the meter moves.

a.c. and d.c. Generators

Turn a coil in a magnetic field and plot the voltage: alternating through slip rings, direct through a split ring. Change the speed, turns and magnet, then work out the frequency.

How a Transformer Works

Change the turns on each coil and watch the changing field in the core induce a bigger or smaller voltage; try a battery instead, then predict the voltmeter reading.

The National Grid

Build the grid with step-up and step-down transformers, send the power, and see why a high voltage means a small current and less heat lost in the cables.

How a Loudspeaker Works

An alternating current in the coil makes the cone vibrate: see a speaker cut in half as in an exam diagram, watch the compressions and rarefactions of the sound wave leave the cone, change the size and frequency of the current, and predict on the picture which way the cone moves.

Unit 7 Radioactivity and Particles

Film Badge: Alpha, Beta or Gamma?

Predict which patches of a film badge go dark for an alpha, beta, gamma or mixed source, then expose it. IAL and IB extras: dose and beta energy.

Random Decay and Half-Life

Nuclei decay one by one at random: watch N halve every half-life on the N–t graph. IAL and IB extras: the ln N–t graph, the decay constant and activity.

Alpha, Beta and Gamma in Fields

Fire alpha, beta and gamma through a magnetic or electric field and see which way each bends, and why. IAL and IB extras: r = p/BQ and the electric field.

Chain Reaction in a Nuclear Reactor

U-235 fuel rods, a moderator and boron control rods: raise and lower the rods and watch the chain reaction die out, stay steady or grow on a graph of fissions per generation, with an Experiment mode.

Penetrating Power of Radiation

Core practical 7.6

Count alpha, beta and gamma through paper, aluminium and lead with a Geiger–Müller tube, and drag the tube away to find the range of alpha in air. Experiment mode: correct for background and identify two mystery sources.
