IGCSE Animations

Unit 1 Forces and Motion

Motion Graphs

Drive a car and watch its distance–time and velocity–time graphs draw, with gradients and areas worked out.

A Toy Car on a Ramp

Core practical 1.5

Release a toy car down a ramp, time it with a stopwatch or light gates, and plot its distance–time and velocity–time graphs.

Force, Mass and Acceleration

Push a trolley with a resultant force and see how force and mass change its acceleration.

Friction

Magnified surfaces rubbing over each other, dry and with a lubricant.

Skydiver

Weight, air resistance and the velocity–time graph, from the jump to two terminal velocities.

Stopping Distance

Two cars stop side by side: tired or distracted drivers, wet or icy roads, and the areas under their velocity–time graphs.

Momentum in a Collision

Two trolleys stick together or bounce apart; total momentum before equals total momentum after.

Hooke’s Law

Core practical 1.22

Load a spring, a metal wire or a rubber band and watch the force–extension graph, past the limit of proportionality.

Moments on a Seesaw

Change two weights and their distances until the clockwise and anticlockwise moments balance.

Unit 5 Solids, Liquids and Gases

Heating Curve and Particles

Core practical 5.11P

Heat ice until it becomes steam: the temperature–time graph and the particles move together.

Specific Heat Capacity

Core practical 5.14P

Heat water or a metal block with an immersion heater and joulemeter, and compare how much each warms up.

Gas Molecules and Pressure

Molecules hit the walls of a cylinder; change the temperature and volume, then see the gas-law graphs.

Pressure in a Liquid

Water pushing on a dam gets stronger with depth, and a U-tube manometer measures gas pressure.

Unit 6 Magnetism and Electromagnetism

Magnetic Field Patterns

Core practical 6.6

Sprinkle iron filings round one magnet, like poles or unlike poles, then check with field lines and a plotting compass.

Fields Around Wires and Coils

Sprinkle iron filings round a straight wire, one loop, a flat coil and a solenoid, then reverse the current and check with a plotting compass.

The Motor Effect

Predict which way a current-carrying wire moves in a magnetic field, then check with Fleming’s left-hand rule.

DC Motor

Step a motor’s coil round a turn at a time: forces on each side, the turning effect and the split-ring commutator.

Magnet and Coil

Push a magnet into a coil and pull it out: what makes the induced voltage bigger, and what reverses it.

The a.c. Generator

Turn a coil in a magnetic field and plot the alternating voltage; change the speed, turns and magnet, then work out the frequency.

How a Transformer Works

Change the turns on each coil and watch the changing field in the core induce a bigger or smaller voltage; then try a battery instead.

Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field

Fire electrons or positive ions into a magnetic field: see when they curve, which way, and why there is no force along the field.

How a Loudspeaker Works

An alternating current in the coil makes the cone vibrate: change the size and frequency of the current and see the sound wave change.
