Friction

A block is pushed along the floor at a steady speed. Below, the place where they touch is magnified many thousands of times: surfaces that look smooth are covered in bumps.

Dry Surfaces

Magnified view
Push neededlarge
Temperature rise0 °C
Bumps touching now0
  • The bumps catch on each other, so the block has to be lifted up and over them. It moves in jolts.
  • Friction acts on the block in the opposite direction to its motion. At a steady speed the push balances it.
  • Work done against friction heats the surfaces and wears the bumps away.

With a Lubricant

Magnified view
Push neededsmall
Temperature rise0 °C
Bumps touching now0
  • A layer of oil keeps the surfaces apart, so the bumps no longer catch on each other.
  • The oil layers slide over each other easily, so friction is much smaller and a smaller push keeps the block moving.
  • Less heating and less wear: why engines and bike chains are oiled.
