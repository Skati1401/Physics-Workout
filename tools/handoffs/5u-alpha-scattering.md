## What the animation holds

- **The experiment.** A vacuum chamber seen from above: alpha source in a lead block, thin gold foil, a detector (zinc sulfide screen and microscope) that is dragged round the foil from 0° to 150°, with a scale of angles on the wall. Play fires 1000 alpha particles a second (Fast forward: 20 000): the beam goes on through the foil, each one deflected by more than 10° is drawn, and four boxes count the fired, under 10°, 10° to 90° and over 90° (1 in 8000). The note gives the observation and its conclusion for where the detector is: straight on (most undeflected, so mostly empty space), small angle (a few deflected, so a concentration of charge), back (very few over 90°, so most of the mass in a very small nucleus). **IAL says the nucleus "is charged"; IB says "small, dense and positively charged"**, as each board's mark schemes do (WPH14 October 2020 Q14(b) has the scattering unable to show the sign of the charge).
- **Model of the atom: Nuclear | Plum pudding.** With Plum pudding every alpha goes almost straight on and the graph shows Thomson's prediction.
- **Graph:** number detected per minute against angle of scattering, Linear (the exam's sharp peak at 0°) or Log (to see the large angles), with both models' curves.
- **Experiment.** The detector moves in 5° steps from 10° to 150°; Count for 1 minute puts the reading in a table and a cross on the graph. After 6 readings that reach 30° or less and 120° or more, both models' curves appear dashed with a conclusion.
- **Show working (the experiment):** the fraction over 90° from the counts; why a vacuum; why a thin foil; what the detector is.
- **Near a nucleus.** One nucleus and the alpha particle drawn to scale in femtometres (gold, silver or copper, with its nuclear notation). The alpha is aimed by dragging it or with the slider (0 to 80 fm off-centre, either side), then Fire: it follows its real path with the electrostatic force on it drawn as an arrow. Fire a beam sends nine at once. Kinetic energy 4.0 to 9.0 MeV at IAL, 4.0 to 40.0 MeV at IB. Beside it: electric potential energy, kinetic energy and total energy against the distance from the centre of the nucleus, with d marked.
- **IB only:** the radius of the nucleus R = R₀A^(1/3) is a reading and is marked on the energy graph; an alpha whose path reaches R is stopped there with a note on the strong nuclear force and deviations from Rutherford scattering (Try these: High energy).
- **Show working (near a nucleus):** E<sub>k</sub> in joules, then the distance of closest approach head-on, substituted before rearranging: IB from E<sub>k</sub> = kq₁q₂/r, IAL from V = Q/4πε₀r and E<sub>k</sub> = qV. IB adds R = R₀A^(1/3) and the kinetic energy at which r = R.
- **Questions (on the picture, then Go):** `path` (click the path alpha Y follows, given X's), `draw` (drag the end of Y's path), `where` (click the detector position), `change` (IB only: more, less or the same deflection when the energy, the nuclear charge or the isotope changes).

**IB (IB Physics Question Index, spec point E.1)**

1. Two observations of the experiment (Describe, 2 marks): P2 HL May 2025 TZ3 Q5(b)(i) R (SL Q3(b)(i) R). The mark scheme's three: most alpha particles pass through undeflected; some are scattered or deviated from their path; a few bounce back from the foil.
2. What the results show, or Rutherford's model (Outline 2, Describe 2 to 5): P2 HL May 2025 TZ3 Q5(b)(ii) R; P2 May 2018 TZ2 (SL Q6(a), HL Q9(a)); P2 HL November 2010 QA5(a); P2 HL May 2004 TZ2 QB4 part 2(b)(iii). Points: the nucleus is small and dense; it is positively charged; most of the mass and all the positive charge are in a very small volume; most of the atom is empty space; electrons orbit the nucleus.
3. **IAL (IAL Past Paper Index, spec point 112; WPH14 unless it says otherwise)**

1. Observations and the conclusion from each (State 6, Describe 5 or 6*, or complete a table of 3): June 2022 Q16(a) R; January 2024 Q14 R; October 2025 Q19(a) R; June 2026 Q15 R; January 2026 (A) Q17(a) R; 9PH0/03 June 2023 Q4. The chain, in pairs: most alpha particles were undeviated, so most of the atom is empty space; a few were deflected through small angles, so there is a concentration of charge in the atom; very few were deviated by more than 90° (or came straight back), so most of the mass is concentrated in a small region, the nucleus.
2. From the plum pudding model to the nuclear model (Discuss or Explain, 6*): January 2020 Q15(b) R; October 2021 Q13(a) R; June 2026 (A) Q16 R. Points: alpha particles were expected to go straight through, or with only a small deflection; a small number were deflected through very large angles or came straight back; so the model changed to an atom with a very small nucleus, mostly empty space; the nucleus contains almost all the mass; the nucleus is charged.
3. How the results showed a small, concentrated central charge (Explain 2), and why they could not show whether it was positive or negative (Discuss 4: a nucleus of either sign explains every observed path): October 2020 Q14(a) and (b) R.
4. The apparatus: why a vacuum (State 1: so the air does not stop the alpha particles reaching the detector), January 2020 Q15(a) R; why a very thin foil (Explain 2: few layers of atoms, so less chance of more than one deflection, or of being absorbed), October 2021 Q13(b) R; why alpha rather than beta or gamma (Explain 3), October 2022 Q15(a) R.
5. Multiple choice, most often "which is not a valid conclusion" (that the nucleus contains protons and neutrons): June 2021 Q2; January 2022 Q8; October 2022 Q2; January 2023 Q5; June 2023 Q8; October 2023 Q5; June 2024 Q7; October 2024 Q9; October 2025 (A) Q10; January 2026 Q7 (all R). Why some are deflected by up to 180°: January 2025 Q5 R. Where most are detected and the path of a second alpha particle: in the animation's Questions (`where`, `path`).
6. Distance of closest approach (indexed under the electric potential spec points, not 112): June 2022 Q16(b)(i) R (4 marks: MeV to J, V = Q/4πε₀r, W = VQ, r); October 2020 Q14(c) R (3 marks).
