# 9f Which isotope? Scenarios from the past papers (school account, 22 Nov 2026)

A game for IGCSE 7.14 (with 7.5, 7.12, 7.15, 7.16), IB E.3 (its guidance names medical isotopes, leaks in underground pipes, thickness of materials and radioactive dating) and IAL 138 and 142. The student gets a scenario, picks one isotope from four to six cards (emission, half-life, sometimes solid or gas), presses Check, and then builds the reason from word chips in the mark schemes' words. One page, an IGCSE | IAL | IB switch: IAL and IB add a half-life calculation to some scenarios (activity left after the treatment or the storage time, with A = A₀e^(−λt)).

Read from the three question indexes on the school Drive (22 Nov 2026). **Reserved papers:** rows marked Mock or Topic test are kept for Alex's tests. Use their wording and the same kind of scenario, but never the same question (its setting with its numbers). Make up the isotopes' half-lives and settings, as the papers do.

## The scenarios and what the mark schemes reward

| # | Scenario | Right choice | Mark scheme words for the reason | Papers (index rows) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A tracer to image an organ (brain, kidney, blood flow) | Gamma emitter, half-life of hours | gamma penetrates out of the body to be detected; alpha and beta are absorbed in the body (short range) and are more ionising, so more harmful; half-life long enough for the scan, short enough to fall to a safe level quickly | IGCSE Jan 2012 P1 6(c); May 2015 P1 TZ1 11(b); Jan 2020 P1 11(b) (TT); May 2024 P1R 8(c) (TT); IAL WPH05 Oct 2018 18(b) |
| 2 | Why the tracer is made at the hospital | (follow-up) | it decays quickly, so most would be gone before it arrived | IGCSE Jan 2020 P1 11(b)(i) (TT) |
| 3 | Treat a thyroid or a small tumour from inside the body | Beta emitter taken up by the organ | beta has a short range / is ionising; absorbed by the thyroid; kills the tumour cells only; alpha not penetrating enough, gamma passes straight through, beta stays within the tumour | IGCSE Jan 2014 P2 1(b); May 2011 P1 13(d) |
| 4 | A treatment that lasts several weeks: pick the half-life | Half-life of a few months (not hours, not years) | activity falls with time; stays active through the treatment, then decays | IGCSE May 2011 P1 13(e) |
| 5 | Beams from outside the head aimed at a brain tumour | Gamma | penetrating; reaches the tumour from several directions | IAL WPH05 June 2016 Q2 |
| 6 | Sterilise packaged instruments or fruit in a box | Gamma, long half-life | gamma more penetrating, passes through the packaging; the fruit is irradiated, not contaminated, and is not made radioactive; long half-life so the source is replaced less often (IAL: time until it must be replaced) | IGCSE May 2019 P1 TZ1 3(a), 3(b); IAL WPH05 June 2017 16(a); WPH15 Oct 2021 19(b) (TT) |
| 7 | Smoke alarm | Alpha, long half-life | smoke absorbs alpha, fewer reach the detector, so the count falls; beta would pass through the smoke and the casing; short half-life means it needs replacing and the count falls on its own; alpha has a range of a few cm in air, so no hazard outside the alarm | IGCSE May 2018 P1 TZ1 10(d), 10(e)(ii); IAL 6PH05 June 2010 14(a), 14(c); WPH05 June 2014 18(c)(ii) |
| 8 | Measure the thickness of paper or aluminium foil on rollers | Beta, long half-life | alpha is stopped by the sheet; gamma passes straight through unaffected; beta is partly absorbed, and how much depends on the thickness; longer half-life, so it stays active and is replaced less often | IGCSE Jan 2013 P2 1(b); Nov 2010 SS 10(c) |
| 9 | Date an object (wood, bone, rock, mud) | Half-life comparable with the age | too many half-lives, so the activity is too low to measure (compared with background); half-life far too short for archaeological samples | IGCSE May 2018 P1 TZ2 12(d); Jun 2005 SS 6(b); IAL WPH05 June 2016 19(d); WPH15 Jan 2026 19(b)(ii) (Mock); IB P2 HL 06 May TZ1 B3 |
| 10 | A school experiment that needs a steady count for an hour | Long half-life | the activity must stay (nearly) constant during the investigation | IGCSE May 2015 P1 TZ2 12(b)(vii) |
| 11 | Follow food through the digestive system | Gamma tracer, swallowed | gamma emitted and detected outside the body; follow its path | IGCSE Jun 2008 SS 7(b) |
| 12 | Find a leak in an underground pipe | Gamma, half-life of hours or days | gamma penetrates the soil to reach a detector above; the count is higher where the tracer leaks out; short enough half-life not to stay in the water supply | IB E.3 guidance (no index question found; write it in the same words) |
| 13 | Which waste is the bigger long-term problem | Longer half-life | stays radioactive for longer, so long-term storage; strong, secure containers | IB P2 HL 04 May TZ1 B3(d)(ii); IGCSE Nov 2021 P1 4(e) (TT); Jan 2021 P1R 10(d) (TT) |

Contamination against irradiation (IGCSE 7.15: May 2019 P1 TZ1 3(b), May 2022 P1 5(c) (TT), May 2026 P1R 10(c)(i) (Mock)) fits as a follow-up chip on scenarios 6 and 7.
