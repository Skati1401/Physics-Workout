# Workout and practical page rules

Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 20 Nov 2026 (word for word) so that animation chats don't load them. Read this file before building or changing a workout, a unit's Units and Key Terms page, a set page or a practical page. `CLAUDE.md` still holds the rules for every page (shell, design tokens, layout, equations, animation, checks).

## Workout size and core practicals

- **How big a workout is (Alex, 16 Oct 2026).** One IGCSE workout per spec sub-section (the spec's own lettered blocks, e.g. 1(b) Movement and position), merging a small sub-section into its neighbour so no page has fewer than 5 tabs. **5–8 short tabs per page, 1 or 2 activities on each (Alex, 5 Nov 2026)**, each page starting with its Warm Up tab and ending with its Sum Up tab (see "Warm Up and Sum Up" below): many small tabs beat a few full ones. **15–30 minutes per workout (Alex, 5 Nov 2026):** the content tabs come to 15–30 minutes in all, not counting the unit's Units and Key Terms page (about 10 minutes more). Core practicals are never part of a workout (see "Core practicals"). **What the 2 really limits (Alex, 11 Nov 2026):** activities a student cannot see without scrolling below the first screen. Two small activities side by side (an `.act-pair`, e.g. a Predict and a Sort, or two Orders) count as one row, so a tab may hold 3 or 4 when they sit two to a row and the tab still fits about one laptop screen (1366×657). A third activity that would sit out of sight below the others is split into its own tab, or the weakest activity is cut. (About 8–16 activities per page; Calculate cards have 2 questions (see "Calculate questions" under Equations), sorts 6–8 cards, matches 4–6 pairs.) Cut KS3-level items and anything a second activity already tests before cutting calculations, which are the most exam-useful. A tab's short multiple-choice questions go in one Quick check card, not a row of single Predicts. **Units and Key Terms: one page per unit (Alex, 12 Nov 2026; before that two tabs on the unit's last non-TA workout).** Each unit's whole-unit Units and Key Terms activities live on their own page, `unit-n-units-and-terms.html`, titled "Unit n Units and Key Terms Workout", with three tabs (two on Unit 5): "Units" (id `units`, heading "Unit n Units": one quantity–symbol–unit activity, plus any unit-conversion activity; no equations-in-words activity (Alex, 11 Nov 2026: the equations are given in the exam)) then "Key Terms" (id `keywords`, heading "Unit n Key Terms": a Match listing every crossword word with its meaning; Alex, 12 Nov 2026: its own tab, the place students check when stuck) then "Crossword" (id `terms`, heading "Unit n Crossword": the unit's one crossword, 8–13 words with TA-only words marked "(TA)" in the clue; the match and the crossword hold the same words, and only real key words (Alex, 12 Nov 2026): a term students must define or use in an answer, not a part, a device, a unit or a verb, so no National Grid, core, turbine or becquerel); Unit 5 has no crossword, so its "Key Terms" tab (id `terms`) is its key-terms match. These pages are the one exception to the 5-tab minimum. Their card sits at the end of the unit's workouts on `igcse.html` (Unit 1's in the Part 2 grid). TA-only words and units get a TA tag, and an activity from a TA page gets a TA badge in its title (`.act-title b.ta`). No workout has a Units or Key Terms tab: every workout's old `#units`/`#terms` links forward to its unit's page (a `try{const to={…}}` line just before `STORE_KEY`), and each unit page's `readSaved()` copies answers saved under the old workout's key once (`FROM_KEYS`). A new unit gets its own page the same way. Where a whole sub-section is Triple Award only, keep it on its own page with a TA tag on its card, so Double Award students can skip the page. ## Tabs and activity layouts (from "Page shell")

- Tabs: a `TAB_IDS` array drives everything, and `applyTabUI(tab)` shows the active panel. To rename or reorder tabs, edit only the nav buttons and `TAB_IDS`; leave the `<section>` blocks where they are.
- Split a tab into two specifically named tabs once it is doing two unrelated jobs, or once it holds more than 2 rows of activities, or the extra ones sit out of sight below the first screen (Alex, 5 and 11 Nov 2026: two side by side count as one row; a unit's Key Terms page may hold 3) (e.g. Fluids became Pressure & Depth + Manometer; Gas Motion became Gas Motion + Gas Pressure).
- **Order inside a tab:** Explore or Watch first (see the idea), then the concept checks (Predict, Sort, Match, Order, Label the graph), then Calculate. Don't repeat the same simulation in two tabs; keep it in the tab where it does most work.
- **Use the width; put things side by side.** On wide screens:
  - Two activities of the same small kind in a row sit side by side in an `.act-pair` (`grid-template-columns:repeat(auto-fit,minmax(min(100%,420px),1fr))`), stacking on narrow screens. Two parallel Order chains can pair. Predicts no longer need a partner (4 Nov 2026): a tab's short multiple-choice questions go in one Quick check card (see "How big a workout is").
  - Calculate questions sit two to a row (`.calc-inputs` grid, `minmax(min(100%,520px),1fr)`), each in its own light box with the answer box beside the question.
  - Explore/slider diagrams are capped (`.sim.sim-small`, `--simw` 340–420px) so the readings sit alongside instead of the picture filling half the page. Reveal pictures inside a Predict stay at about 200px.
  - Label the graph puts the caption pool in a column to the right of the graph (stacking below it under about 860px).
  - Spot the Error examples sit in a grid of cards about 400px wide.
- **Saving answers and progress (21 Oct 2026).** Every workout and practical saves each activity's answers in `localStorage` (this browser only), keyed by a `STORE_KEY`, with every read and write wrapped in try/catch, and renders normally when nothing is saved. In the `act()` engine (Units 4–6 and the practical template) each graded factory returns `track(host,{getState,setState,isDone})`; saves wait a second after any click or input (so wrong answers have bounced back first) and run again on `pagehide`. Write any new graded factory the same way. Year 9 Heat Transfer's `readSaved()` also converts answers saved by its older version (`heat-transfer-lab-v1`). A tab gets a ✓ once all its graded activities are complete (an activity hidden in its tab, such as another method type's, doesn't count; Memory and explores aren't graded), and the eyebrow line shows "4 of 7 tabs done". Both are worked out from the saved answers. Bump `STORE_KEY` (and set `OLD_STORE_KEY` to the old one, or `oldStoreKey` in a practical's `page.json`) when questions change, so old saved answers are not loaded into new questions. Do not use any `window.claude` code; it doesn't exist outside claude.ai, so remove it if you find it in an older page.
- Every activity has a "Shuffle Again" (or reset) button so a teacher can rerun it with the next class.

## How activities behave

- **Drag and drop** uses custom mouse and touch events (not HTML5 drag and drop) via `makeDraggable`, with a 6px threshold to tell a tap from a drag.
- **Tentative, then Check.** Never mark an answer on drop. A "Check" button reveals results: correct ones lock and turn green; wrong ones shake, then return to the pool after 700–800ms. Students must be able to get it wrong and see it. **Show answer (Alex, 4 Nov 2026):** after a Check with a wrong answer, every marked activity (fill in the blanks, units tables, labels, sort, match, order, crossword, plot) shows a "Show answer" button (`answerBtn()` after `bar()` in the engine). It fills in and locks the right answers, dashed (`.is-shown`) rather than green, and counts as done. A Calculate question never opens its working by itself: a wrong answer gives that question its own Show answer button, which puts the answer in the box and opens the working. Predicts already show the answer after a second wrong try. **Fill in (Alex, 5 Nov 2026):** every `createBlankGame` activity (fill-in-the-blanks passages, units tables, graph and diagram labels) has a Check button, Shuffle again and Fill in (`createBlankGame({fill:true,…})`); Fill in is always visible, does what Show answer does, and replaces Show answer on those activities. Passages label their Check button "Check answers".
- **One click empties a slot.** Clicking any filled, unlocked slot sends its chip straight back. Dropping a new chip on a filled slot replaces it.
- **Reorder** updates the order live while dragging and also has ▲▼ buttons. Check gives a score, not per-row marks.
- **Don't give the answer away.** Labels and chips describe the physics meaning ("Constant velocity, backwards"), never a visual feature the student can match by eye ("sloping down"). Ask: could a student get this right by matching words instead of understanding? If so, reword it.
- **Keyboard and screen readers.** Every chip, card and slot is a `<button>` (or has `tabindex="0"` and `role="button"`). Select-then-target works with Enter and Space as well as clicks. Keep a visible `:focus-visible` outline. Announce Check results in an `aria-live="polite"` region (e.g. "6 / 8 correct"). Touch targets are at least 44px tall.

## Calculate questions (from "Equations")

- **Calculate working is two columns (Alex, 5 Nov 2026).** Write a Calculate question's `working` as `"givens||steps"`: the known values down the left, one per line (`s = 18 m`, a unit conversion on its own line, `t = 10 min = 600 s`), then the unknown as `a = ?`; after `||`, the equation as learnt, the numbers substituted, each rearranging step, then the answer. `createCalc` lines up each column on its = signs and stacks them on phones. A pure read-off with no equation can stay one column. Models: the 1c suvat card and the 2a P = I × V card.
- **Calculate questions (Alex, 11 Nov 2026).** A Calculate card normally has **2 questions**, and New problems gives a new pair with different values and different properties (the context, the material or object, and which quantity is the unknown). **Question 1 always needs a rearrangement**: the unknown is never the subject of the equation as learnt (find m or V from ρ = m/V, never ρ). **Question 2 is harder**: a power or root (r in Snell's law with sin⁻¹, v from KE = ½mv² or v² = u² + 2as), a unit change (minutes to seconds, cm² to m², g to kg), a prefix (k, M, m, G), a minus sign, or two equations in a row; give it `hints`. Write each question as a generator `rnd=>({q,unit,answer,working,hints})` that first picks a variant (which quantity is unknown) and a context, then the values from lists (`calcPick`) so answers are tidy, and build the card with `calcFix(gen, seed)`, which makes the first (fixed) set from the generator and adds it as `make` (e.g. `calcAct(tab,title,intro,[calcFix(q1,1),calcFix(q2,2)])`; models: 1a Speed and acceleration, 1d Balance the beam with its diagram drawn inside each question so New problems redraws it). Write numbers with `calcNum()` (no float noise, real minus sign, spaced thousands) and give a needed `a`/`an` from the number (an 8 kg, an 18 V). Cards whose numbers come from one fixed graph, table, trace or animation (Read the graph, Work out the numbers, Resistance from the graph, stopping distance, the nuclear-equation balances) are exempt and keep their questions. The engine saves a signature of each card's questions and generators with its answers, so a changed card never loads old answers into new boxes: no `STORE_KEY` bump is needed for a Calculate change. Every Calculate question has a `make: rnd=>({q, unit, answer, working})` that writes a new version testing the same skill (same equation, rearrangement and unit conversion), with values chosen from lists (`calcPick(rnd, […])`) so answers are tidy and sensible, and varied contexts and names. The card then shows a New problems button; the first set is always the fixed questions, and the seed is saved with the answers. A question whose numbers come from a fixed graph, table, diagram or animation gets no `make`. **Hints on demand (6 Nov 2026):** give a harder multi-step question `hints:[…]` (one step per hint, through `FR()`, and from `make` too); `createCalc` then shows a "Hint (1 of 3)" button under it that reveals one more hint per press (model: 4c Energy and Power); pages that use it need the `.calc-hint-row`/`.calc-hints` CSS from `work-and-power.html`. Check every page with `python3 tools/calc-check.py page.html` (local server on port 8765), then read some generated problems yourself: the checker only checks they are consistent, not that the physics is right.

## Warm Up and Sum Up (Alex, 12 Nov 2026)

Every workout (IGCSE and Year 9) starts with a **Warm Up** tab and ends with a **Sum Up** tab. The first tab keeps its old id (`overview`, or `fill` on Year 9 Heat Transfer), so old links still work.
- **Warm Up:** one question worth thinking about (the hook) beside an "In this workout: By the end you can" list (`goalsAct`: one short line per tab or spec group, with its spec numbers and a TA tag on mixed pages), side by side (`pairFirstTwo`). The hook is a `createPredict` labelled Predict for a real "what happens if" question and Multiple choice otherwise; it previews the topic's main idea or misconception, needs no content the student has not met, and its reveal explains the physics. No fill-in passage on this tab: a cloze on a topic not yet learned is guessing.
- **Sum Up:** short exam-style word boxes (`wordBox`, kind "Word box": "Use words from the box to complete the sentences. Some words are not needed."), usually two side by side, each 3–5 gaps with 2–3 extra words that are real misconceptions, checked like any fill-in (every chip against every gap). A TA-only box gets a TA badge in its title. A key-words activity that sat on the old Overview (Year 9) moves here.
- A page's start-up code drops a saved fill-in passage under `<first tab>-0`, so it is never loaded into the Warm Up question.

## Spec points and Triple Award

- **Every tab starts with the same heading.** A `.panel-head` holding an `<h2>` title (Big Shoulders, title case) and a `.spec` list of the tab's 4PH1 spec numbers as small badges, e.g. `<div class="panel-head"><h2>Momentum</h2><div class="spec"><b class="ta" title="Triple Award only">TA</b><b>1.25P</b><b>1.27P</b></div></div>`. Warm Up and Sum Up can say "All of 5a"; Key Terms and Units can leave the badges out. Take the numbers from the spec itself, never from memory: if you don't have the relevant page of the spec, ask Alex for it rather than guessing.
- **Mark Triple Award content as TA.** Any tab covering a P spec point (e.g. 1.25P–1.33P, 5.8P–5.14P) gets the TA badge first in its spec list and a TA tag on its tab button (`data-ta` attribute, drawn by `.tab-btn[data-ta]::after`). Add `<span class="head-note">TA = Triple Award only</span>` after the `<h1>`.

## Balance against the spec

Before a topic page is finished, list its spec points and check each has at least one activity, that no tab is spent on something the spec doesn't name (Unit 5's Manometer tab became two questions), that no idea is repeated by three activities, that each spec section gets a similar number of tabs, and that every named practical has an Order activity.

**Drawing the exam diagrams (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** Every workout whose spec points need a diagram drawn (ray diagrams, field lines, equipotentials, free-body and vector diagrams, Sankey, HR, circuit, standing-wave and space–time diagrams) has a task where the student draws or completes that diagram; a framed animation's draw task counts. The list by spec point for IGCSE, IAL and IB is in `tools/DRAWING-DIAGRAMS.md`. Model each task on how the past exam questions ask for it (what is given, what the student adds, how it is marked), taking the question kinds from the past papers or Alex's question indexes, never from memory. ## Pitching it

- **Primary (about 7–11):** 3–5 tabs, one activity each; 4–6 items per sort or match; short words; pictures on chips; chips at least 48px tall with 16px+ text; warmer success animation; no scores that compare pupils.
- **KS3 (11–14):** the Heat Transfer page's level; 6–10 items per activity.
- **GCSE/IGCSE and above:** the Forces page's level; exam-style wording, numeric questions, graphs, units tables.
- Keep wording at the class's reading level. Every scenario should be a plausible exam phrasing, not a giveaway. If a test or scheme of work is shared, use it only to see which skills to target; never copy its questions.

## Group activities

For team tasks (jigsaws, escape rooms), design so the group genuinely has to cooperate: each member holds information the others need, and answers are checked in a way that can't be guessed or copied from one person.
