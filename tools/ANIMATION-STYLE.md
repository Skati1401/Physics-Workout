# Animation style: the motor and generator model (10 Nov 2026)

Alex wants every teaching animation good enough to teach from on a projector. `animations/dc-motor.html` and `animations/ac-generator.html` are the model (Alex, 10 Nov 2026: "use it as the model", with the label rule below). The batches are in `TODO.md`, section 8. Alex calls one up by number ("do animation batch 3").

## What a batch chat does

1. Read `CLAUDE.md`, this file, and the two model pages. Load the `teaching-animations` and `interactive-revision-lab` skills.
2. For each page in the batch: open it at 1366×657 and at phone width, play it, and judge it against "The standard" below.
3. Rebuild or restyle what falls short and push straight to `main` (Alex, 10 Nov 2026). **Ask Alex first only where there is a real choice** (two sound ways to show the physics, dropping a feature, a different kind of animation altogether): one question, options one line each, recommendation first.
4. A page that is already at the standard is left alone; say so in the report.
5. Never change the physics, readings, notes, working, `?embed`, `?show=` or `?level=` behaviour unless it is wrong. If something is wrong, fix it and say so plainly in `tools/REVIEW.md`.
6. Run the checks below. Set each changed page's row in `tools/REVIEW.md` to "🔍 To review" with what to look at. Tick the batch in `TODO.md`.
7. Report to Alex: one line per page (left alone, restyled, redrawn, or what you asked), with two or three screenshots.

Another chat may be pushing to the repo: `git pull --rebase` before every push, and one batch per page at a time.

## The standard

- **The picture is the apparatus, drawn as a solid object.** Blocks with a lit top, a front and a darker side; round wires with a lighter streak; real parts (brushes, rings, clamps, meters) where the old page had a symbol or a caption. No flat outline boxes standing in for equipment.
- **Show the exam diagram beside it.** Where the exam uses a flat diagram of the same thing (end-on coil with dot and cross, circuit diagram, ray diagram, graph), show it next to the picture at the same moment, not behind a switch.
- **One wide picture.** `viewBox` about 860×350, so the picture fills the left column of the stage card at 1366×657. Under 520px wide the same `draw()` switches to a tall `viewBox` (430 wide) with the parts stacked, and the SVG gets class `tall` (no height cap).
- **Things that move in the physics move in the picture.** Current is shown by small arrowheads travelling along the wire, + to − (`flowMarks`, colour `--cur`); they stop when the current stops and turn round when it reverses. Meter needles follow the reading.
- **Colour carries meaning and repeats.** N red, S blue (solid, white letter), field teal, force orange, current purple. Field lines drawn on a mid-grey object (an iron core) are bright with a dark edge (`--fld` over `--fld-edge`, as in `transformer.html`), because plain teal does not show on grey; a stronger field has more lines. Two parts that must be told apart get two colours and keep them everywhere they appear (coil side A copper, side B grey, and the same colours on their ring halves, slip rings and end-on symbols).
- **Labels never lie over the picture (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** Put each label in clear space next to the part it names, with a short leader line and a panel-coloured halo (`txt(…,{halo:1})`). Labels that move with a part (A, B) sit where nothing else is drawn. Check every position of a full cycle, not one frame.
- **A wave or a pattern of particles is drawn flat, in the exam diagram (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** In the 3D kit's camera a flat sheet of dots slopes away from the apparatus and reads as wrong perspective (the loudspeaker's air did). Draw it straight on, in line with the part that makes it, and label its features as they travel (`air()` in `loudspeaker.html`: compressions, rarefactions, one wavelength).
- **Text is readable from the back of a room:** 12–14 in `viewBox` units for labels, never under 11.
- **Predict on the picture, then Go (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** Where a rule gives a direction or a position (Fleming's left-hand rule, induced current, which way a ray bends, where the image forms), the page has a question mode: a button deals a question in the diagram, the student clicks on the diagram where or which way they think (an arrow, a spot, a pole), then presses Go and the animation shows what really happens and marks the click. No row of answer buttons under the picture when the answer can be given on the picture itself.
- **Build it yourself where the exam asks for it (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** Circuit pages deal a task ("Make a circuit that measures the current in the lamp and the p.d. across it"), let the student add the ammeter and the voltmeter themselves (drag or click into place), and on Go show it working, or what goes wrong when a meter is in the wrong place.
- **Draw the exam diagram yourself (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** Where the page's spec point needs a diagram drawn (ray diagrams, field lines, equipotentials, free-body and vector diagrams, Sankey, HR, circuit, standing-wave and space–time diagrams; the list by spec point for IGCSE, IAL and IB is in `tools/DRAWING-DIAGRAMS.md`), the page has a task where the student draws or completes it on the exam view, at each level the page offers. Model the task on how the past exam questions ask for it (what is given, what the student adds, how it is marked), with question kinds taken from the papers, never from memory.
- **Numbers in the reading boxes sit level (Alex, 10 Nov 2026).** `.stat` is a column (`display:flex;flex-direction:column;justify-content:space-between`), so the number is on the bottom line of every box whether its label takes one line or two. Every animation page has this; keep it when a page is rebuilt.
- Everything in `CLAUDE.md` still holds: fits one laptop screen, buttons under the picture, drag the objects, Experiment toggle, dark mode, reduced motion, no capitals-only text.

## The 3D kit

Both model pages carry the same block of drawing code, marked `3D kit`: `scene3` (camera, back-to-front sorting, `seg`, `tube`, `box`, `poly`, `head`), `rig3` (magnet, coil, split ring or slip rings, brushes, circuit, cell or meter), `endOn`, `flowMarks`, `tri`, `keyRows`, `poleRect`, `wireEnd`. Copy the block into another page and call what you need; pages stay self-contained. A fix to the kit goes into every page that has it (search for `3D kit`).

- Axes: X right, Y away from you, Z up. The camera looks from the front, 14° from the right and 30° from above, so the poles never hide the coil.
- Colour tokens the kit needs (copy all three theme blocks from `dc-motor.html`): `--north-f/-t/-s`, `--south-f/-t/-s`, `--copper-hi`, `--coilb`, `--coilb-hi`, `--cur`, `--insul`, `--brush-t/-f/-s`.
- Directions: `c` is the current in side A (+1 towards you, a dot in the end-on view); `sig` is +1 when the current leaves the coil through the right-hand brush. The split ring is at the front of the coil, so with N on the left and + on the left brush, side A on the right carries current towards you and is pushed up. Work the direction out from F = I l × B or v × B for every new case; do not copy a sign from another page.

## The instrument kit (batch 7, 10 Nov 2026)

`resistivity.html`, `internal-resistance.html`, `photoelectric.html` and `electron-diffraction.html` carry one identical block, marked `instrument kit`, straight after the solid kit (which it needs). The three batch 6 circuit pages carry it too (and the solid kit). It is for pictures of circuits on a bench, drawn straight on. A fix goes into every page that has the block. Batch 6 (the circuit pages) can copy it.

- `dmm(M, o)`: a **digital meter** with a reading on its display (`o.text`, `o.unit`), a letter on the case (A, V) and a terminal on each side; `dmmGeom(M)` gives where the leads plug in. Digital, because these practicals read to 0.001 A and 0.01 V, which a needle cannot show. `M.posts = [x1, x2]` puts the terminals on top instead (a supply standing under what it feeds, as in `electron-diffraction.html`). The same function draws a supply: leave out the letter and colour the terminals with `o.tl` and `o.tr`.
- `lead(points)`: an insulated lead. `hang(a, b, sag)` and `bez(a, c1, c2, b)` give the points of a lead that hangs or curves, so the current arrows can follow it. A voltmeter's leads are the lighter `--lead-v` and carry no arrows.
- `flowPath(pieces, phase, gap, size)`: current arrows along a whole loop given as pieces `[points, visible]`; mark the piece through a meter, a cell or a resistor as not visible and the arrows keep step either side of it. `tri` and `flowMarks` are the 3D kit's.
- `shadow`, `stud`, `rod` and `icase` (a case with a lit top and a darker side) for the rest of the bench. No bench is drawn: each object throws a soft shadow, so the labels between the objects are in clear space.
- `sizeView(svg, wide, tall)` picks the wide (860) or the tall (430) picture from the SVG's width; each page calls it in `layout()` at the start of `draw()`, and a `ResizeObserver` redraws when the shape changes.
- Colour tokens it needs (copy the line marked "instrument kit colours" from all three theme blocks of `resistivity.html`).
- **Layout for apparatus, graph and circuit diagram together** (the two electricity pages): the apparatus fills the left 500 of the 860; the graph is top right and the circuit diagram under it, about 340 × 110. The circuit helper block's `axes()` has larger text in these two pages than in the other electricity pages, for the wider picture.

## The circuit board kit and the Build tab (batch 6, 11 Nov 2026)

`series-parallel.html`, `iv-characteristics.html`, `potential-divider.html`, `resistivity.html` and `internal-resistance.html` carry one identical block, marked `circuit board kit`, after the solid kit, the instrument kit and the circuit helpers (which it needs; all three pages carry those too). A fix goes into all five. `electrostatic-charging.html` has none of the kits: it keeps its own 600 × 300 scene inside a scaled group, with its own `bar()` for a rod.

- **One circuit, drawn twice from the same data.** A circuit is `{cell, nodes, edges, vslots, dots}` in its own units (about 400 × 280). An edge is a wire or a component (`res`, `lamp`, `diode`, `var`, `ldr`, `therm`, or `custom` with its own `bench()` and `diag()`), with its path; a wire may have a `slot` where a meter can go in series, and each `vslot` is a place for a meter across a component. `kBench(P, st, T)` draws it built on the bench (seen from in front and above: every part has a top face, a darker front edge and a soft shadow, and can lie along the wire either way) and `kDiagram(P, st, T)` draws the circuit diagram. `T = {x, y, sx, sy}` places the circuit's units in the picture, so the bench and the diagram match part for part.
- **State** `st`: `placed` (slot: 'A' or 'V'), `cur` and `dist` (current and arrow distance per edge), `text` (meter readings), `glow`, `psu` (the supply's display), `rev` (battery turned round), `slots` (true: empty slots are drawn and can be pressed), `kind(e)` and `name(e)` (what a part is drawn as and labelled), `hi` (ring colours after marking).
- **Layers:** leads, then current arrows, then parts, then meters, so an arrow never lies over a part. `kDiagram` returns the slots separately; put them in their own group and redraw it only when its text changes (`setKeepFocus`), so the keyboard keeps its place while the arrows move.
- **`kSolve`** is nodal analysis with the meters in it: an ammeter is almost no resistance, a voltmeter a very large one (or `o.ra`, `o.rv` for real meters). An ammeter joined straight across the supply is a short circuit: the supply is then given 0.5 Ω of its own, so its p.d. collapses and the rest of the circuit gets nothing. `kReading` is what a meter in a slot reads.
- **Any meter can go in any slot.** Never refuse a wrong placement: show what happens. `kFault` gives the words for a meter of the wrong kind (short circuit, voltmeter in series, ammeter in parallel); `kMark(P, task, …)` marks a task `{A:[slots], V:[slots], aOf, vOf, okNote}` and also catches a meter of the right kind in the wrong place.
- **The Build tab** ("Explore | Build" at the top of the right-hand column): the setup controls are hidden, a task is dealt and written under the diagram, the supply shows "off" and the meters "– – –" until Go. Go switches on, runs the arrows and marks it; moving a meter switches off again. Make one part a lamp, so a wrong meter is seen (the lamp goes out or gets brighter) as well as read. Some tasks start with the meters already in the wrong place ("find the mistakes"). The task kinds come from the Edexcel 2.9 questions (complete the circuit: ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel with the component; find the errors: ammeter in parallel, voltmeter in series).
- **Three things side by side** (bench, diagram, graph) when a page has a graph: about 300, 250 and 230 wide in the 860 picture; the bench and the diagram use different scales of the same circuit. On a phone they go one under the other.
- **Where labels go on the diagram:** an upright component's label goes to the right of its wire, above the voltmeter's lead (`lab:[8,-34,'start',-5]`, which moves with the view's scale), or to its left when the right is taken. Readings are on the bench meters, not on the diagram.
- **Sizes to keep:** a bench meter is 70 × 44 (78 wide where a reading has a minus sign); slots along a wire need about 80 units between a corner and a component, or the meter overlaps them. Check "All meters" on every circuit.
- **A design task instead of meters** (`potential-divider.html`): the student chooses which part goes at the top (press a part on the diagram, or the Sensor buttons), and Go changes the light or the temperature while the graph is drawn.
- **A Build tab on a page that has its own apparatus picture (11 Nov 2026):** `resistivity.html` and `internal-resistance.html` carry a second identical block, marked `Build tab`, straight after the kit. `buildTab({svg, id, P, tasks, V, rOf, kind, names, what, note, labels, after, hide, enter, redraw})` adds “Explore | Build” (the page needs `id-btab`, `id-bchips`, `id-blab` and `id-bnew` in its markup, and the `.chip` CSS). In Build the page's own picture is replaced by the kit's bench and diagram; the page's `draw()` starts with `if(BT && BT.on()){ BT.draw(); return; }`, and its Play, Reset and drag handlers hand over to `BT` the same way. A fix goes into both pages.
- **A cell with internal resistance** (`internal-resistance.html`): the ideal cell is node `P`, its internal resistance is a `custom` edge from `P` to the terminal `T` that draws nothing on the bench and a small resistor in a dashed box in the diagram, and the voltmeter's place across the cell joins `T` and `N`. An ammeter across the cell then reads ε/r without any special case. `cell:{kind:'batt', n:1}` draws one cell, not a battery of two.
- **Arrows only where there is a current:** `kFlow` draws no arrows under 0.02 mA, so a wire with a voltmeter in series, or a by-passed part, is seen to carry no current.
- **Wording by level:** 4PH1 says “voltage”; IAL and IB say p.d. `series-parallel.html` writes its text with “p.d.” and passes it through `pdw()`; `iv-characteristics.html` writes “voltage” and passes it through `vw()`.
- **Short windows:** the Build tab row makes the right-hand column one row taller, so these pages make their choice buttons 38 px tall when the window is under 640 px high.

## Field patterns on a card (batch 1, 10 Nov 2026)

`magnetic-field-patterns.html` and `electromagnet-field-patterns.html` share these pieces (a fix goes into both):

- **The card keeps its own units** (560 × 340, which the physics, filings and Experiment use). `layout()` places it in the wide or tall picture (`L.cx`, `L.cy`, `L.cs`); `cardG()` wraps card drawing in that transform, `cardPoint()` turns a pointer into card units, and the filings canvas uses the same numbers.
- **Filings settle tap by tap:** Sprinkle drops them at random; each **Tap the card** turns every filing a fraction `k` of the way still left (near 1 where the field is strong, about 0.2 where it is weak) and jolts the card. With no field (current off) `k` is 0.
- **The compass needle swings** to the field and settles (`swing()`, a damped spring on the needle's angle, updated in place so a drag is never interrupted). Under reduced motion it turns straight to the field.
- **The exam diagram:** for the magnets, a smaller copy of the card with flat poles and arrowed lines beside it, showing "?" until the filings have been tapped, Field lines is on, or a question is answered. For wires and coils the card seen from above is itself the exam diagram, and the 3D picture beside it shows where the card is.
- **Predict** deals a spot with eight dashed arrows round it (which way the compass's north end points), lettered spots, or the ends of a solenoid or core; only questions whose answer is clear (within 8° of an arrow, or a field at least three times stronger) are dealt.

## Choices: the Choice kit (Alex, 12 Nov 2026)

Students did not see that "Explore | Practise" was a choice, or that the rows of options were pick-one. So every animation page carries the same two blocks, marked `Choice kit`: `<style id="choice-kit">` after the page's own styles and `<script id="choice-kit-js">` after its script. Edit the kit in `motor-effect.html`, then run `python3 tools/choice-kit.py` to copy it into every page (or name a new page to add it there).

- **A group of choices is one track.** Any `.seg` whose buttons have exactly one chosen (`aria-pressed="true"`) gets class `pick` from the script: a tinted track with a thin outline, the chosen button filled, the others plain text. Nothing moves, so the one-screen fit is unchanged. Rows of buttons that do something ("Jump to", "Try these") have none chosen, so they stay as separate buttons. A group where several can be on at once (`.apps`) is left alone too.
- **Every group has a visible label** (`.lab` or `.rowlab`), not only an `aria-label`.
- **Label above, track fitted, short groups side by side (Alex, 13 Nov 2026),** as on `dc-motor.html`. A `.cgrid` of `.rowlab` + control pairs is laid out by the kit: its script wraps each label and the control after it in a `.cgroup` (label above, group only as wide as its buttons) and lets the groups wrap side by side; a group whose label and control are both hidden is hidden. A `.cgrid` with only one group (e.g. Graph under the picture) keeps its label beside the control, to save height. Don't give a choice group a fixed grid of equal-width buttons (the old 2×2 Face block on `heat-transfer.html`). Checked on all 28 pages with a `.cgrid` with `anim-check.py`: no new failures.
- **The mode switch is the main choice,** so it looks different from the settings: `class="seg tabs mode"`, a wider track with larger bold text and the chosen half in ink. It is the first thing in the right-hand column.
- **The question mode is called Questions** on the button, on every page that deals questions ("Explore | Questions"). Circuit pages keep "Explore | Build". Text that tells students what to press says Questions too (workout intros). The prompt in a question can still start "Predict."
