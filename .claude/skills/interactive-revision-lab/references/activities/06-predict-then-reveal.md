# Activity 6: Predict-then-Reveal (interactive-revision-lab)

Shared rules (headings, no capitals, checklist, verification) are in SKILL.md; the engine helpers, layout and equations are in the `workout-page-shell` skill.

Commit-before-you-see. Present a scenario (a short description, plus a small static diagram if useful) and 3-4 multiple-choice predictions as buttons — written to include the common misconceptions, not just one clearly-wrong distractor per obviously-right answer. Student clicks one; that locks in their choice (visually marked, buttons disabled) and immediately reveals the outcome below: an animation or diagram state change (e.g. two force arrows fading in to show a balanced pair), plus 1-2 sentences explaining what actually happens and, if they picked wrong, *why that specific misconception is wrong* rather than just restating the right answer. No drag/drop needed — `makeDraggable` isn't involved, this is button state + a CSS transition/reveal. Keep the options a similar length; if the right answer is the longest or the most detailed, students pick it by length, so even them up. Two Predicts in a row sit side by side (see "Layout" in the `workout-page-shell` skill), and any reveal picture stays small (about 200px). `createPredict` takes `pre` (HTML shown under the question, e.g. a small data table). Forces example: "A book sits on a table, pushed sideways, but doesn't move" → predictions include "no forces are acting", "friction exactly cancels the push", "the push is too weak to count as a force", "gravity is stopping it" → reveal shows a free-body diagram animating in both arrows simultaneously with equal length.
