# Activity 9: Slider Simulation (lower priority — best for IAL/IB-level topics with a clean single-variable relationship) (interactive-revision-lab)

Shared rules (headings, no capitals, checklist, verification) are in SKILL.md; the engine helpers, layout and equations are in the `workout-page-shell` skill.

A slider (`<input type="range">`) bound to one input variable, live-updating a rendered output — a number/readout, a bar, or a point/curve on a simple SVG or `<canvas>` axis — via a real formula (e.g. `V = IR`, a linear or quadratic relationship, force vs. extension). Not graded, no Check button — it's an explore-and-build-intuition tool, so label it as "play with this" rather than a task. Recompute and redraw on every `input` event (not just `change`) so it feels live. Scope this to a genuine single-relationship formula Claude can compute directly (algebra, not a physics engine) — it is not a substitute for a real simulator: no collisions, no multi-body interactions, no rendered 3D. Good candidates are anything GCSE/IAL/IB would express as one equation with one free variable at a time (resistance vs. current at fixed voltage, extension vs. force for a spring within its elastic limit, etc.). Show the equation beside the slider with the current numbers substituted in, using stacked fractions. For an animation that plays along a graph (e.g. the Unit 5 heating curve with particles), let students scrub both ways: drag the point along the graph (pointer events on the SVG, `touch-action:pan-y`) as well as the time slider, keeping the two in step, and put that Watch activity first in its tab.

**Conventions for slider explores** (all built in the physics-workout pages):
- Layout (the 1b momentum collision is the model): the picture on the left with its sliders underneath; on the right, the choice buttons (stick/bounce, road surface, material…), Go and Reset, then the working and a one-line note. On phones the two columns stack. `stackSliders(cardSel, colCls, rowSel, segSel)` arranges this from the markup, and `balanceSims(cardSel, rowSel, segSel)` (ResizeObserver, only above 860px) sizes the picture so both columns end at about the same height, clamped between `data-minw` (default 0.32) and 0.6 of the width, moving sliders to the right column if the left is still more than 60px taller. Give pictures holding graphs `data-minw="0.45"`–`"0.55"` so the graphs stay readable. Goal: use the space, little empty space. On phones shorten button labels and drop the readout font to about 12.5px.
- The readout shows the equation, the numbers substituted into it, then each step of the rearranging on its own line, with the = signs lined up (see "Equations" in the `workout-page-shell` skill), and updates on every `input` event. Draw labels inside the SVG with the page's mono font.
- A Go button for anything that moves (a trolley accelerating, a collision, a lorry crossing a bridge) runs a `requestAnimationFrame` loop that **always ends**: stop at the end of the track or after a fixed time, then show a one-line conclusion ("Total momentum after = … the same as before"). Changing any slider resets the run. **Go runs play even under reduced motion**: they only start when a student presses Go, they are short and always stop, and seeing the motion is the point (many school laptops have animations turned off, which made trolleys jump straight to the end). Reduced motion still turns off decorative effects (shake, fades, flips). A sim that loops continuously (Unit 5 particle boxes) still starts paused under reduced motion, with a Play button.
- **Don't store an animated position in a stepped slider.** A range input with `step="0.5"` rounds every small animation increment back down, so the object never moves. Keep the position in a JS variable, draw from that, and copy it into the slider for display.
- Scale things so the extremes still fit: test the largest and smallest slider values at phone width and check nothing is clipped at the start or end of the track.
- Reference sims to copy: the animation pages (see the table in SKILL.md, "Start from the working code", and the `teaching-animations` skill): motion graphs car (readout = gradient of the stretch it is on + area under the v–t graph), F = ma trolley (10 m track, dots every 0.5 s), skydiver, stopping distance (reference car vs your car on v–t axes, worked by area), momentum collision (both trolleys moving, stick or bounce), Hooke's law (spring, wire, rubber band, with Experiment mode), seesaw, heating curve with particles (draggable dot), gas molecules + gas-law graphs, pressure in a liquid (dam + U-tube manometer). Still inside workouts: Unit 5 pressure (F and A) and specific heat, 1b bridge with a lorry.
