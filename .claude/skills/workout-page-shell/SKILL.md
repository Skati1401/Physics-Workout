# Workout page shell, engine, animation and equations

Shared by `interactive-revision-lab` and `teaching-animations`. Read the shell, engine and animation sections when building a new page from scratch or changing how the shell, the engine or the movement works; for an edit to an existing page, that page's own code is the shell, so copy from it. Read the equations section for any page that shows an equation or a calculation.

## Page shell

- **Structure**: `<title>`, Google Fonts links, one big `<style>` block, then `<div class="page">` containing a `<header class="page-head">` (a breadcrumb line + a small `<h1>`, no lede paragraph — it just eats space; see **Header** below), a `<nav class="tabs" role="tablist">` of short 1-2 word tab labels, then one `<section id="panel-{id}" class="panel" hidden>` per activity (the first/default tab has no `hidden`), then a single `<script>` IIFE with all data, helpers, activity factories, and wiring.
- **Header (Alex, 21 Oct 2026)**: keep it to one compact line, like Google Drive's path. A breadcrumb `<nav class="eyebrow crumbs" aria-label="Breadcrumb">` where every level is a link and each is followed by `<span class="sep" aria-hidden="true">›</span>` (physics-workout site: "Workouts › Year 9 ›" to `index.html` and `year-9.html`; "Workouts › IGCSE › Unit n ›", the unit linking to `igcse.html#unit-n`; practicals end "› Core practicals ›" to `igcse.html#unit-cp`), then a small `<h1>` (22–30px) on the same line, then the tabs-done pill (the engine appends it to `.page-head`) and any `.head-note`. No separate back link and no eyebrow line of course/year/topic text. A `.key` legend or `.cp-link` line wraps onto its own row below. Crumb links are at least 44px tall. Copy the "compact breadcrumb header" CSS block from `motion.html` (the practical template has it in `style.css`). Artifacts outside the site use the same pattern with whatever levels exist (or just the title and pill if there are none).
- **Tabs on phones**: at ≤640px the tab nav is one row that swipes sideways (`flex-wrap:nowrap; overflow-x:auto`, hidden scrollbar, a fade on the right edge to show there is more), not 4–5 wrapped rows. `applyTabUI` scrolls the open tab into view in that row. The tabs scroll away with the page; they are not pinned.
- **Tabs**: `TAB_IDS = ['id1', 'id2', ...]` array drives everything — `applyTabUI(tab)` just hides every panel whose id isn't the active one and toggles `.tab-btn.is-active`/`aria-selected`. Reordering or renaming tabs later is just editing the nav buttons' visible text/order and this array to match; the panels' physical DOM order doesn't need to match and isn't worth the edit risk to change — leave existing `<section id="panel-...">` blocks where they are and only touch the nav + `TAB_IDS`.
- **Order the activities inside a tab** the same way every time: Explore or Watch first (see the idea), then the concept checks (Predict, Sort, Match, Order, Label the graph), then Calculate, with Spot the Error last, straight after the Calculate it builds on. Students learn where things are, and a teacher can say "do down to Calculate" on any tab. Don't repeat the same simulation in two tabs; keep it in the tab where it does most work and remove the copy.
- **Split a tab once it outgrows one clear activity** (or holds more than 2 activities, Alex 5 Nov 2026: Unit 5's Fluids became Pressure & Depth + Manometer, and Gas Motion became Gas Motion + Gas Pressure). A tab that's accreted two or three unrelated tasks under one label (e.g. a "Newton's Laws" tab that ended up with a match, a units table, *and* worked examples) is a sign to split it into separate, specifically-named tabs ("Force Definitions" vs "Free Body Diagram", "s–t Graph" vs "v–t Graph") rather than keep stacking `.section-label` dividers inside one panel. This is cheap: create the new `<section id="panel-newid">`, move the relevant HTML block into it, add one nav button, add the id to `TAB_IDS`, and re-point that activity's `addReflow`/game registration at the new panel id. Do this proactively when a tab is clearly doing two jobs, not just when asked.
- **How big a workout is**: One IGCSE workout per spec sub-section, merging a small one into its neighbour so no page has fewer than 5 tabs. **5–8 short tabs per page, 1 or 2 activities on each (Alex, 5 Nov 2026)**, each page with its own Overview: many small tabs beat a few full ones. **15–30 minutes per workout:** the content tabs come to 15–30 minutes in all, not counting the unit's Units and Key Terms tabs (about 10 minutes more). Core practicals are never part of a workout (see the next point). A tab that would need a third activity is split into two tabs, or its weakest activity is cut (about 8–16 activities per page). **Units and Key Terms: two tabs per whole unit (Alex, 2 Nov 2026; split into two tabs 5 Nov 2026)**, not one per workout: they are the last two tabs of the unit's last non-TA workout, so Double Award students see them too (Unit 1 → 1c, Unit 2 → 2c, Unit 3 → 3b, Unit 4 → 4c, Unit 5 → 5b, Unit 6 → 6b, Unit 7 → 7b, Unit 8 → 8a). "Units" (id `units`, heading "Unit n Units") holds one quantity–symbol–unit activity for the whole unit, plus any unit-conversion or equations-in-words activity; "Key Terms" (id `terms`, heading "Unit n Key Terms") holds the unit's one crossword (TA-only words marked "(TA)" in the clue) or a key-terms match. Each follows the 1–2 activity rule. An activity from another workout of the unit is titled after it ("1d Key terms"), and one from a TA page gets a TA badge in its title; TA rows in a units table get a TA tag. The other workouts have no Units or Key Terms tab. A sub-section that is all Triple Award gets its own page with a TA tag on its card. Build new units to this from the start; igcse.html groups cards under a heading per unit.
- **Core practicals sit outside the workouts (Alex, 7 Nov 2026)**: they appear in lots of places and are large, so a workout never frames a practical's animation or holds its practical activities. The workout tab for that spec point may carry a one-line link to the practical page, nothing more. On `igcse.html` each unit's section lists its workouts first, then that unit's core practical pages as separate cards. The last section, Core Practicals (`#unit-cp`), only points to those cards in the units; it holds no second copy. (Not done yet on the live site as of 7 Nov 2026: the existing workouts still frame their practicals; the move is in `TODO.md`, section 5. Until it is done, the repo's CLAUDE.md describes the current layout.)
- **Design tokens**: define once in a bare `:root` (light), then repeat under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark){ :root:not([data-theme="light"]){...} }` and again under `:root[data-theme="dark"]{...}`. Core tokens: `--bg, --bg-grid, --panel, --panel-border, --ink, --ink-soft, --ink-faint, --accent, --good/-soft, --bad/-soft, --shadow`. Give each topic category its own color pair (e.g. `--cond/-soft`, `--conv/-soft`) reused consistently for that category's icon, bin, and any related reference card — this is what makes cross-tab content feel connected (e.g. an "insulator" token reused between a conduction-sort tab and an insulation-summary card).
- **Fonts**: Big Shoulders (headings), IBM Plex Sans (body), IBM Plex Mono (labels/badges/numbers) — via Google Fonts.
- **Layout**: `.page{max-width:1400px; margin-inline:auto; padding-inline:20px;}`. Year 9 mostly use Chromebooks, where the page just fills the window; IGCSE mostly use laptops (about 1280–1536px), where the extra width means cards wrap less and students scroll less on short screens. In a match game whose left items are short terms, give the left column less width (`minmax(0,1fr) minmax(0,2.6fr)` on wide screens) so each definition fits on one line and the whole list fits on screen. Panel intro paragraphs must NOT have a `max-width`/`ch` constraint — that leaves a dead blank column on wide panels. Any small decorative diagram should float small (≤130px) or sit in its own full-width strip above the content — never big enough to push the actual activity below the fold.
- **Use the width: put things side by side.** On wide screens nothing should be a full-width strip of short content:
  - Two small activities of the same kind in a row sit side by side in an `.act-pair` (`display:grid; grid-template-columns:repeat(auto-fit,minmax(min(100%,420px),1fr)); gap:18px; align-items:start`), stacking on narrow screens. Every Predict gets a partner (write a second one if a tab has only one); two parallel Order chains can pair too.
  - Calculate questions sit two to a row (`.calc-inputs{display:grid; grid-template-columns:repeat(auto-fill,minmax(min(100%,520px),1fr))}`), each row in its own light box with the answer box beside the question.
  - Explore/slider diagrams are capped (`.sim.sim-small` with `--simw` 340–420px) so the readings sit beside the picture. Reveal pictures inside a Predict stay at about 200px.
  - Label the graph puts the caption pool in a column to the right of the graph, stacking below it under about 860px.
  - A graph with questions about it sits beside them (`.graph-split`: graph about 560px left, questions right, stacking under about 900px), and so does graph paper with the table being plotted (`.plot-split`).
  - Spot the Error examples sit in a grid of cards about 400px wide.
  - Bar readouts (`.vfill`) get `min-width:max-content` so a short bar never cuts off its label.
  - A short data table is only as wide as its content (`width:fit-content`), not a full-width strip.
- **Uniform card/item height, correctly**: when a set of draggable cards or sort-items must all match height (e.g. a match activity's left column, force-type chips), set a flat pixel `min-height` sized for the worst-case content (longest wrapped text at the smallest font-size in play), not an `em`-based value. With global `box-sizing:border-box` (used throughout), an em value computed off a card's own font-size can come out *smaller* than that card's own padding+border+content already requires — so it silently does nothing, and short one-line cards stay visibly shorter than wrapped two-line ones. Verify with a screenshot, not just by reading the CSS.
- **Per-viewer state**: every activity is independent client-side state — safe for many students to use the same link simultaneously. **Saving answers:** every graded activity factory returns `track(host,{getState,setState,isDone})`, so its answers are saved in `localStorage` (try/catch) and its tab gets a ✓ when done; the header shows 'N of M tabs done'. Bump `STORE_KEY` and set `OLD_STORE_KEY` when questions change. It never syncs between students, which is what a class needs. Do **not** use `window.claude.hot.snapshot/ready`: it is not part of the artifact runtime (`window.claude` only exposes `use()`), so the older labs' feature-detected calls silently do nothing. When updating one of those labs, swap that code for `localStorage`. If a lab ever needs shared or teacher-visible results (a class leaderboard, a submitted score), load the artifact-capabilities skill first; that needs the `db` capability, not browser storage. Every activity needs a "Shuffle Again" (or equivalent reset) button so a teacher can re-run it with the next class. A factory whose `setState` writes values straight onto existing DOM (rather than re-deriving them from a `render()` call) should NOT also expose `render` to the generic post-restore render loop — that would immediately overwrite the just-restored state; only expose `render` when it's safe to call after `setState`.
- **Tab counts (physics-workout site):** the GoatCounter snippet in every page's head defines `pwCountTab(tab, label)`, and `applyTabUI` calls it, so each tab a student opens is counted once per visit as an anonymous event (`page#tab`), skipped in frames and with `?embed`. Copy the snippet and `applyTabUI` from an existing page and new pages get this for free.

## Shared interaction engine

- **Drag and drop**: custom mouse and touch events (not HTML5 DnD) via a `makeDraggable(el, {onStart, onMove, onEnd, onClick})` helper — works for mouse and touch. Use a 6px movement threshold to tell a tap from a drag. On drag, clone the element into a `.drag-ghost` that follows the pointer via `transform: translate(...)`; on release, find the drop target with `document.elementFromPoint(x,y).closest(...)`.
- **Tentative + Check pattern (use for every graded activity)**: placements/pairs/order are never validated instantly on drop. A "Check ..." button reveals correctness: correct ones lock (turn green, stop being interactive); incorrect ones flash red (`.is-wrong` + a `shake` keyframe) then bounce back to their start after ~700-800ms via `setTimeout`. This is essential — never let the UI silently block or auto-correct a wrong placement, since students need to be able to get it wrong and see that.
- **Every filled-but-unlocked slot must be a single click away from empty, always.** Whatever the placement mechanic — a labelled blank, a bin, a graph drop-marker — clicking a slot/item that's currently filled (and not yet locked-correct) must immediately clear it back to the pool/bank, with no extra step. Don't gate removal behind a "select it, then click empty space elsewhere" flow (that reads as broken — a real bug found in testing: a sort-bin item's click handler only *selected* it, requiring a follow-up click precisely on the pool's own background to actually release it). Re-dropping a new chip directly onto an already-filled slot should also just overwrite it. This is what lets a student who dropped something in the wrong place fix it without hunting for the right gesture.
- **Reorder-specific**: don't validate the drop position at all — update the array continuously as the dragged item crosses another row's midpoint ("live reorder"), so whatever order it's released in is already the current state. This is the only way to genuinely allow a wrong order to be dropped.
- **Reusable palette for a small fixed answer-set used across many items** (e.g. 5 arrow directions applied across 9 force-balance diagrams, or any activity where the same handful of category chips get reused many times rather than consumed one-for-one): build the palette/bank ONCE, not on every `render()` — rebuilding it each render tears down and reattaches `makeDraggable`, which cancels an in-flight drag and is unnecessary churn since the palette itself never changes. On drop, *copy* the chip's value into the slot rather than removing it from the bank/pool, so any chip can be dragged again immediately. Prefer this over a 1:1 consumable pool whenever the underlying answer set is small and naturally repeats (contrast with a topic-vocabulary sort, where each pool item is a distinct term used exactly once and a consumable pool is correct).
- **Don't give the answer away**: bin/category labels, and any descriptive draggable chip, must describe the *outcome or meaning*, not repeat or paraphrase a literal visual/descriptive feature already visible in what's being labelled (e.g. a bin sorting "matt/dark" surfaces should be labelled by what happens — "Absorbs & Emits Well" — not by the surface description already stated in the card; a chip labelling a section of a graph should say what's physically happening there — "Constant velocity, backwards" — never a shape word like "curving upward" or "sloping down", since that lets a student match by eyeballing the picture instead of reasoning about the physics). When in doubt, ask: could this be solved correctly by pattern-matching words instead of understanding the content? If yes, reword it.

- **Every activity must work without a mouse.** Drag is the fast path, not the only one. Make every draggable chip, card and slot a real `<button>` (or give it `tabindex="0"` and `role="button"`) and support the tap-to-select-then-tap-target flow the sort and match factories already have, triggered by Enter/Space as well as a click. Keep a visible `:focus-visible` outline. Announce Check results in an `aria-live="polite"` region (the score pill is a good host) so a screen-reader user hears "6 / 8 correct". Reorder already has ▲▼ buttons; give sort, match, fill-in-the-blank and graph-labelling the same keyboard path. Touch targets are at least 44px tall.

## Animation

Movement is feedback, not decoration: it tells a student what just happened. Use CSS transitions and `@keyframes` only (no animation library).
- **What to animate**: a correct lock (a short scale-up to 1.05 and back, plus the green fill fading in, about 250ms); a wrong answer (the existing `shake`, about 500ms, then the bounce back to the pool); a chip settling into its slot (a 150ms ease-out on `transform`); a panel appearing (the existing 250ms `fadein`); predict-then-reveal outcomes (arrows growing from zero length, a diagram state change over 400–600ms); a finished activity (one brief celebration, e.g. the score pill pulsing, never a full-screen effect that hides the work).
- **Timing**: keep feedback under about 600ms so it never delays the next attempt. Leave the wrong-answer bounce-back at 700–800ms so the student sees what was wrong first.
- **Only animate `transform` and `opacity`** so it stays smooth on old school Chromebooks and phones.
- **Respect reduced motion.** Wrap every decorative or large movement in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, and under `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` swap the shake for a colour change and the flips and slides for an instant state change. The meaning (correct, wrong, revealed) must still come through without the movement.
- **Nothing loops forever** and nothing flashes more than three times a second.

## Equations: stacked fractions, never ÷

Never show a division in an equation with a ÷ sign; it makes rearranging harder to follow for no reason. Write every fraction as `[[top|bottom]]` in the data and pass the text through a small helper that draws it stacked:

```js
const FR=t=>String(t).replace(/(\d) (?=\d{3}\b)/g,'$1 ')   // keep "15 000" on one line
  .replace(/\[\[([^|\]]+)\|([^\]]+)\]\]/g,'<span class="frac"><span>$1</span><span>$2</span></span>');
```
```css
.frac{display:inline-flex;flex-direction:column;align-items:center;vertical-align:middle;margin:0 .15em;line-height:1.25}
.frac>span{padding:0 .25em;white-space:nowrap}
.frac>span:first-child{border-bottom:1.5px solid currentColor}
```
Use it everywhere an equation appears: Spot the Error steps, Calculate intros and "Show full working", equation readouts beside sliders (`el.innerHTML=FR('p = [[F|A]]<br>p = [['+F+'|'+A+']] = …')`), Predict feedback and Order steps. Where a button's text contains a fraction, give it an `aria-label` that reads the fraction as "top over bottom".

**Substitute, then rearrange (Alex, 13 Oct 2026).** Whenever a readout or worked answer needs a rearranged form, write the equation as students learn it, substitute the numbers into that form, and only then rearrange for the unknown, one step per line, then the answer. Never rearrange the symbols first. Do **not** add notes such as "divide both sides by 3": the lines themselves show the algebra. **No × in algebra unless it is between two numbers** (IGCSE/KS3; Alex, 10 Nov 2026): `F = ma`, `p = mv`, `3v`, `2500a`; keep × between numbers and in word equations.
```
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v
2 × 3 + 1 × 0 = (2 + 1)v
6 = 3v
v = [[6|3]]
v = 2 m/s
```
For braking distance from v² = u² + 2as, state v = 0 and acceleration −a, substitute the numbers (0² = 20² + 2 × (−5)s), then rearrange (10s = 400 → s = [[400|10]] = 40 m) (only in the "Using v² = u² + 2as" tab and in questions with no graph; see the next rule).

**Motion: use the graph, not the equations of motion.** Wherever a motion graph is on screen (an animation, an explore, a Calculate question with a graph), the working reads the answer off the graph, as students are taught to: speed or acceleration = gradient of the relevant stretch, written as [[change in velocity|time taken]] with the two points read off the graph; distance = area under the velocity–time graph (rectangle = thinking distance, triangle = ½ × base × height = braking distance, total area = stopping distance). In a live readout, read the times off the graph first ("brakes on at 0.7 s, stops at 3.56 s"), then work the areas. For a "what if the speed doubles" question, draw both journeys on one graph (the old one dashed) and compare the shapes: the rectangle is twice as tall (× 2), the triangle twice as tall and twice as wide (× 4).

**Use a real minus sign.** A number formatter like `num()` gives a hyphen ("-13"); wrap it (`sn(v)` → `'−'+num(-v)`) for every negative value in working, notes and slider outputs, and put negative values inside brackets when substituted (1 × (−3)).

**Keep fractions narrow enough for a phone.** A fraction never wraps (`white-space:nowrap`), so a long top such as "p(gas) − p(air)" pushes the page sideways at 380px. Name it first (Δp = p(gas) − p(air)), then substitute into Δp = h × ρ × g (4000 = h × 1000 × 10), then rearrange (h = [[4000|1000 × 10]]), which also reads better, and drop the working to about 12px under 420px.

**Line up the = signs.** Every working readout reads left | = | right, like a worked answer on paper. `watchEquals(sel)` (in all three IGCSE pages) uses a MutationObserver to turn each `<br>`-separated line into a grid row of `.eqg-l | = | .eqg-r` (lines with no " = " span the row as `.eqg-full`), so readouts that re-render on every `input` stay aligned.

**Keep the "why it's wrong" explanations.** The no-notes rule is only about algebra steps. Spot the Error and Predict feedback must still explain why a chosen wrong answer is wrong.
